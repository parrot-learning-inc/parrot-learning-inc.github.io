# Basic verbs in Spanish: the 30 you need first

These 30 basic verbs in Spanish cover most of what you say in a normal day: being, having, going, doing, wanting, and talking. Below you get each verb in the present, past, and future, with the irregular ones marked. Learn ser, estar, tener, ir, and hacer first. They do the most work.
{: .lede }

You do not need to know 500 verbs to speak Spanish. A small set of verbs shows up again and again, and the rest of the language hangs on them. This page gives you that set, plus the patterns that make thousands of other verbs easy.

## The 30 most useful basic verbs

How to read the table:

- **Type** tells you how much memorizing you need. **Irregular** verbs have forms you just have to learn. **Stem change** verbs follow a pattern (more on that below). **Regular** verbs follow the endings table.
- **yo (past)** is the preterite, for finished actions: ayer **comí** (yesterday I ate).
- **yo (future)** is the simple future. In everyday speech, people more often say **voy a** + infinitive: voy a comer (I'm going to eat).

| Verb | Meaning | Type | yo (present) | tú | él / ella | yo (past) | yo (future) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ser** | to be (identity, origin, time) | **Irregular** | soy | eres | es | fui | seré | Soy de Chicago.<br>*I'm from Chicago.* |
| **estar** | to be (location, feelings, states) | **Irregular** | estoy | estás | está | estuve | estaré | Estoy un poco cansado.<br>*I'm a little tired.* |
| **tener** | to have | **Irregular** | tengo | tienes | tiene | tuve | tendré | Tengo dos hermanos.<br>*I have two brothers.* |
| **ir** | to go | **Irregular** | voy | vas | va | fui | iré | Voy al trabajo en metro.<br>*I go to work by subway.* |
| **hacer** | to do / to make | **Irregular** | hago | haces | hace | hice | haré | Hago la cena los viernes.<br>*I make dinner on Fridays.* |
| **poder** | can / to be able to | **Irregular** | puedo | puedes | puede | pude | podré | ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?<br>*Can I pay by card?* |
| **decir** | to say / to tell | **Irregular** | digo | dices | dice | dije | diré | Siempre digo la verdad.<br>*I always tell the truth.* |
| **querer** | to want / to love | **Irregular** | quiero | quieres | quiere | quise | querré | Quiero un café, por favor.<br>*I want a coffee, please.* |
| **saber** | to know (facts, how to) | **Irregular** | sé | sabes | sabe | supe | sabré | No sé nadar.<br>*I don't know how to swim.* |
| **ver** | to see / to watch | **Irregular** | veo | ves | ve | vi | veré | Veo series en español.<br>*I watch shows in Spanish.* |
| **dar** | to give | **Irregular** | doy | das | da | di | daré | Te doy mi número.<br>*I'll give you my number.* |
| **venir** | to come | **Irregular** | vengo | vienes | viene | vine | vendré | Vengo de la playa.<br>*I'm coming from the beach.* |
| **poner** | to put | **Irregular** | pongo | pones | pone | puse | pondré | Pongo las llaves en la mesa.<br>*I put the keys on the table.* |
| **salir** | to leave / to go out | **Irregular** (yo) | salgo | sales | sale | salí | saldré | Salgo de casa a las ocho.<br>*I leave home at eight.* |
| **conocer** | to know (people, places) | **Irregular** (yo) | conozco | conoces | conoce | conocí | conoceré | Conozco un bar muy bueno.<br>*I know a really good bar.* |
| **pensar** | to think | Stem change e → ie | pienso | piensas | piensa | pensé | pensaré | Pienso mucho en el viaje.<br>*I think about the trip a lot.* |
| **volver** | to come back / to return | Stem change o → ue | vuelvo | vuelves | vuelve | volví | volveré | Vuelvo a casa a las siete.<br>*I get back home at seven.* |
| **dormir** | to sleep | Stem change o → ue | duermo | duermes | duerme | dormí | dormiré | Duermo ocho horas.<br>*I sleep eight hours.* |
| **pedir** | to ask for / to order | Stem change e → i | pido | pides | pide | pedí | pediré | Siempre pido lo mismo.<br>*I always order the same thing.* |
| **entender** | to understand | Stem change e → ie | entiendo | entiendes | entiende | entendí | entenderé | No entiendo la pregunta.<br>*I don't understand the question.* |
| **empezar** | to start / to begin | Stem change e → ie | empiezo | empiezas | empieza | empecé | empezaré | Empiezo a trabajar a las nueve.<br>*I start work at nine.* |
| **hablar** | to speak / to talk | Regular | hablo | hablas | habla | hablé | hablaré | Hablo un poco de español.<br>*I speak a little Spanish.* |
| **comer** | to eat | Regular | como | comes | come | comí | comeré | Como a las dos.<br>*I eat lunch at two.* |
| **vivir** | to live | Regular | vivo | vives | vive | viví | viviré | Vivo cerca del centro.<br>*I live near downtown.* |
| **trabajar** | to work | Regular | trabajo | trabajas | trabaja | trabajé | trabajaré | Trabajo desde casa.<br>*I work from home.* |
| **tomar** | to take / to drink | Regular | tomo | tomas | toma | tomé | tomaré | Tomo el autobús todos los días.<br>*I take the bus every day.* |
| **llegar** | to arrive | Regular (spelling change: llegué) | llego | llegas | llega | llegué | llegaré | Llego tarde otra vez.<br>*I'm late again.* |
| **necesitar** | to need | Regular | necesito | necesitas | necesita | necesité | necesitaré | Necesito ayuda.<br>*I need help.* |
| **aprender** | to learn | Regular | aprendo | aprendes | aprende | aprendí | aprenderé | Aprendo español con videos.<br>*I learn Spanish with videos.* |
| **gustar** | to like (literally: to please) | Special (me gusta) | me gusta | te gusta | le gusta | me gustó | me gustará | Me gusta la playa.<br>*I like the beach.* |

Two things jump out. First, ser and ir share the same past form: **fui** can mean "I was" or "I went". Context tells you which. Second, look at gustar: it does not work like the others. "Me gusta la playa" means "the beach pleases me". The thing you like is the subject, so you say **me gusta** for one thing and **me gustan** for more than one.

🎯 **Key point:** You can talk about the future with one verb you already know. **Voy a** + any infinitive: voy a trabajar, vas a ver, va a llover. It is the easiest tense in Spanish.

## Regular endings for -ar, -er, and -ir verbs (present)

Most Spanish verbs are regular. To conjugate one, remove the -ar, -er, or -ir and add the ending for the person. Hablar becomes habl-, then hablo, hablas, habla.

| Person | -ar | -er | -ir | hablar | comer | vivir |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yo | **-o** | **-o** | **-o** | hablo | como | vivo |
| tú | **-as** | **-es** | **-es** | hablas | comes | vives |
| él / ella / usted | **-a** | **-e** | **-e** | habla | come | vive |
| nosotros | **-amos** | **-emos** | **-imos** | hablamos | comemos | vivimos |
| vosotros (Spain) | **-áis** | **-éis** | **-ís** | habláis | coméis | vivís |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **-an** | **-en** | **-en** | hablan | comen | viven |

[Download the endings table as CSV](/data/basic-verbs-in-spanish-endings.csv).

Notice that -er and -ir verbs share every ending except nosotros and vosotros. So you really learn two and a half sets, not three. **Vosotros** (you all, casual) is only used in Spain. In Latin America, people say **ustedes** for any group, casual or polite, and use the ellos form: ustedes hablan.

## Boot verbs: the stem-changing verbs

Some verbs change the vowel in their stem (the part before the ending) when that vowel gets the stress. **Poder** becomes **puedo**, not podo. The endings stay regular.

The change happens in yo, tú, él/ella, and ellos, but not in nosotros or vosotros. If you write the six forms in a grid and draw a line around the ones that change, it looks like a boot. That is why teachers call them boot verbs.

| Change | Verb | Meaning | yo (changes) | nosotros (no change) | ellos (changes) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e → ie | **pensar** | to think | pienso | pensamos | piensan |
| e → ie | **querer** | to want | quiero | queremos | quieren |
| e → ie | **entender** | to understand | entiendo | entendemos | entienden |
| e → ie | **empezar** | to start | empiezo | empezamos | empiezan |
| e → ie | **cerrar** | to close | cierro | cerramos | cierran |
| e → ie | **preferir** | to prefer | prefiero | preferimos | prefieren |
| e → ie | **sentir** | to feel | siento | sentimos | sienten |
| o → ue | **poder** | can | puedo | podemos | pueden |
| o → ue | **volver** | to come back | vuelvo | volvemos | vuelven |
| o → ue | **dormir** | to sleep | duermo | dormimos | duermen |
| o → ue | **encontrar** | to find | encuentro | encontramos | encuentran |
| o → ue | **recordar** | to remember | recuerdo | recordamos | recuerdan |
| u → ue | **jugar** | to play | juego | jugamos | juegan |
| e → i | **pedir** | to ask for | pido | pedimos | piden |
| e → i | **repetir** | to repeat | repito | repetimos | repiten |
| e → i | **servir** | to serve | sirvo | servimos | sirven |

[Download the stem-changing verbs as CSV](/data/basic-verbs-in-spanish-stem-changes.csv).

There are four kinds of change: **e → ie** (pensar, pienso), **o → ue** (poder, puedo), **e → i** (pedir, pido), and one special case, **u → ue**, which is jugar (juego). Dictionaries often show the change in brackets, like pensar (ie).

The change is a present-tense thing. In the preterite, -ar and -er verbs go back to normal: pensé, volví. Only -ir verbs keep a small change in the él and ellos forms: durmió, pidió.

⚠️ **Watch out:** Do not use the stem change in nosotros. It is **podemos**, not "puedemos", and **queremos**, not "quieremos". This is the most common boot-verb mistake.
{: .warn }

## The 5 verbs to learn first

If you only have one week, spend it on these five. They show up in almost every conversation, and they are all irregular, so the earlier you start, the better.

1. **Ser** (to be: who or what). Soy Ana. Soy de Texas. Es la una. Our [ser conjugation guide](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/ser-conjugation?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=basic-verbs-in-spanish) has every tense.
2. **Estar** (to be: where and how). Estoy en casa. Estoy cansado. ¿Cómo estás? See the full [estar conjugation](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/estar-conjugation?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=basic-verbs-in-spanish). Still mixing the two up? This guide to [ser vs estar](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/ser-vs-estar?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=basic-verbs-in-spanish) sorts it out.
3. **Tener** (to have). Tengo dos hijos. It also covers things English does with "to be": tengo hambre (I'm hungry), tengo 30 años (I'm 30).
4. **Ir** (to go). Voy al trabajo. Plus **voy a** + infinitive for the future, which makes ir two verbs in one.
5. **Hacer** (to do, to make). ¿Qué haces? Hago la cena. It also talks about weather: hace calor (it's hot), hace frío (it's cold).

With these five plus the infinitive trick (quiero comer, puedo ir, voy a hablar), you can already say a lot. The trick works because only the first verb changes. The second one stays in the infinitive, so you can use any verb on this page before you learn its forms.

## How to practice verbs without boring yourself

- **Learn forms inside sentences.** "Tengo hambre" sticks. "Tengo, tienes, tiene" in a list does not.
- **Start with yo and tú.** In real conversations, you mostly talk about yourself and the person in front of you. The other forms can wait a week.
- **Use flashcards for the irregular forms only.** Regular verbs follow rules. Save your card time for soy, voy, tengo, and hice. Our [Spanish flashcards](/spanish-flashcards/) page has a free deck and import steps.
- **Then add the past.** When the present feels easy, learn the preterite. The [preterite vs imperfect guide](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/preterite-vs-imperfect?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=basic-verbs-in-spanish) explains which past tense to use.
