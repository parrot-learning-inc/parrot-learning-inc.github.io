# Free Spanish lessons: a 30-day beginner course

These free Spanish lessons take you from "hola" to a one-minute talk about yourself in 30 days. Each day has 5 key words or phrases, one grammar point, and a 2-minute speaking task. Plan on about 20 minutes a day.
{: .lede }

## How the course works

Every lesson builds on the one before, so go in order. Days 7, 14, 21, and 28 are review days: no new topic, just the words and grammar from the past week, used together. If a day feels hard, repeat it tomorrow. Nobody is grading you.

Brand new to Spanish sounds? Spend 10 minutes on the [alphabet in Spanish](/alphabet-in-spanish/) before day 1. Spanish spelling is very regular, so once you know the sounds you can read almost any word out loud.

🎯 **Key point:** The 2-minute task is the most important part of each lesson. Saying things out loud, even badly, is how the words move from "I recognize it" to "I can use it."
{: .key }

## The 30-day course

| Day | Topic | 5 key words or phrases | Grammar point | 2-minute task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greetings | hola, buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches, adiós | *Buenos días* but *buenas tardes*: the word matches the noun (*día* is masculine, *tarde* and *noche* are feminine) | Greet the people in your day out loud: morning, afternoon, and night |
| 2 | Your name | me llamo, ¿cómo te llamas?, mucho gusto, encantado / encantada, soy | *Soy* = I am, *eres* = you are (verb *ser*) | Say your name three ways (*me llamo*, *soy*, *mi nombre es*), then ask "¿Cómo te llamas?" |
| 3 | Where you are from | ¿de dónde eres?, soy de, vivo en, la ciudad, el país | *Ser de* + place for where you are from | Say where you are from and where you live, then ask the question back |
| 4 | Numbers 1 to 10 | uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco | *Uno* becomes *un* before a masculine noun: *un café* | Learn *seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez*, then count 1 to 10 and back |
| 5 | Polite words | por favor, gracias, de nada, perdón, lo siento | Put *no* before the verb to make it negative: *no sé*, *no hablo* | Say "Perdón, no hablo mucho español" five times, a little faster each time |
| 6 | Things around you | la casa, la mesa, el libro, el teléfono, la puerta | *El* for masculine nouns, *la* for feminine. Most -o nouns are masculine, most -a nouns are feminine | Name 10 things in your room with *el* or *la* (look up the ones you do not know) |
| 7 | **Review: days 1 to 6** | hola, me llamo, soy de, gracias, la casa | *Ser* so far: *soy, eres, es* | Introduce yourself in 5 sentences with no notes, and record it |
| 8 | Family | la familia, la madre, el padre, el hermano, la hermana | Plurals: add -s after a vowel (*hermanos*), -es after a consonant (*ciudades*) | Name your family members out loud, then say each word in the plural |
| 9 | How are you? | ¿cómo estás?, bien, mal, cansado / cansada, contento / contenta | *Estar* for feelings: *estoy, estás, está* | Ask "¿Cómo estás?" and answer it five different ways |
| 10 | Age and having | tengo, ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo hambre, tengo sed, el año | *Tener*: *tengo, tienes, tiene*. Spanish uses *tener* for age, hunger, and thirst | Say your age and three things you have (for example *un perro*, *una hermana*) |
| 11 | Question words | ¿qué?, ¿dónde?, ¿cuándo?, ¿quién?, ¿por qué? | Question words take an accent in questions, and questions open with ¿ | Write 5 questions for a new friend, then say them out loud |
| 12 | Everyday -ar verbs | hablar, trabajar, estudiar, escuchar, necesitar | Present of -ar verbs: *hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, hablan* | Say 5 things you do every week with these verbs |
| 13 | Describing people | alto / alta, simpático / simpática, joven, grande, pequeño / pequeña | Adjectives usually go after the noun and match it: *una casa pequeña* | Describe a friend in 4 sentences |
| 14 | **Review: days 8 to 13** | la familia, estoy, tengo, ¿dónde?, hablo | Three key verbs: *soy* (who you are), *estoy* (how you are), *tengo* (your age) | Describe one family member: who they are, how they are, and how old they are |
| 15 | Food and drink | comer, beber, el agua, el pan, el café | Present of -er and -ir verbs: *como, comes, come*; *vivo, vives, vive* | Say what you eat and drink on a normal day |
| 16 | Likes | me gusta, no me gusta, me encanta, ¿te gusta?, la música | *Me gusta* + one thing, *me gustan* + more than one: *me gustan los perros* | Name 3 things you like and 2 things you do not like |
| 17 | Plans | voy a, mañana, esta noche, el fin de semana, ¿qué vas a hacer? | *Ir a* + verb for the near future: *voy a comer* | Say 3 things you are going to do this weekend |
| 18 | In town | hay, el banco, la farmacia, el baño, cerca de | *Hay* = there is or there are, for one thing or many | Describe your street: what there is and what there is not |
| 19 | At a café | quiero, quisiera, la cuenta, para llevar, ¿cuánto cuesta? | *Quisiera* is a softer, more polite *quiero* | Role-play ordering a coffee and paying. Play both the customer and the server |
| 20 | Telling time | ¿qué hora es?, es la una, son las dos, y media, de la mañana | *Es la una* for one o'clock, *son las* for every other hour | Say what time it is now, then the times of 4 things in your day |
| 21 | **Review: days 15 to 20** | me gusta, voy a, hay, quisiera, son las | Verb + infinitive: *voy a comer*, *quiero comer* | Role-play a full café visit: greet, order, ask the time, pay, and say goodbye |
| 22 | My things | mi, tu, su, mis amigos, nuestro / nuestra | *Mi* and *tu* add -s for plural: *mis libros*, *tus amigos* | Describe 5 things you own with *mi* or *mis* |
| 23 | Asking for help | ¿puede repetir?, más despacio, no entiendo, ¿cómo se dice...?, ¿qué significa...? | *Poder* + verb: *¿puedes ayudarme?* Use *puede* with *usted* to be polite | Say all 5 phrases until they come out without thinking. You will use them every day |
| 24 | Daily routine | me levanto, me ducho, desayuno, me acuesto, temprano | Reflexive verbs use *me, te, se*: *me levanto* = I get up. In much of Latin America you also hear *me baño* for "I shower" | Describe your morning in 5 steps |
| 25 | Ser vs estar | es alto, está cansado, es médico, está en casa, es lunes | *Ser* for what something is (identity, job, day), *estar* for states and location | Make 3 sentences with *ser* and 3 with *estar* about yourself |
| 26 | Days of the week | lunes, miércoles, viernes, hoy, ayer | *El lunes* = on Monday (no word for "on"). Days are lowercase in Spanish | Learn *martes, jueves, sábado, domingo*, then say one thing you do each day |
| 27 | Yesterday | ayer, hablé, comí, fui, vi | Preterite for finished actions: -ar verbs end in -é (*hablé*), -er and -ir verbs in -í (*comí*). *Fui* means "I went" and "I was" | Say 5 things you did yesterday |
| 28 | **Review: days 22 to 27** | mis, ¿puede repetir?, me levanto, está, ayer | Present vs past: *hablo* (I speak) vs *hablé* (I spoke) | Tell the story of yesterday, morning to night, in 6 sentences |
| 29 | Weather | ¿qué tiempo hace?, hace calor, hace frío, hace sol, llueve | *Hacer* for weather: *hace frío* = it is cold (word for word, "it makes cold") | Describe today's weather and your favorite weather |
| 30 | Put it together | y, pero, porque, también, entonces | Join short sentences with *y, pero, porque* to sound natural | Record a 1-minute talk about yourself with all 5 words. Then play your day 7 recording and compare |

## How to do each lesson in 20 minutes

| Minutes | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Warm up | Review yesterday's 5 words. Say each one in a short sentence |
| 3 to 8 | New words | Read today's 5 words or phrases. Hear each one (SpanishDict and Forvo have audio), then say it out loud 3 times |
| 8 to 11 | Grammar point | Read the grammar point, then make 3 sentences with it. Short and simple is fine |
| 11 to 18 | Listening | Watch or listen to something short on the same topic, at your level. Notice today's words when you hear them |
| 18 to 20 | 2-minute task | Do the task out loud. On review days, record yourself |

A few sounds to get right in week 1, since you will say these words every day:

| Spanish | Sounds like | Note |
|---|---|---|
| hola | OH-lah | The *h* is always silent |
| gracias | GRAH-see-ahs | In most of Spain: GRAH-thee-ahs |
| buenos días | BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs | The accent on *días* marks the stressed syllable |
| mucho gusto | MOO-choh GOOS-toh | *Ch* sounds like the English "ch" in "church" |
| ¿cómo estás? | KOH-moh ehs-TAHS | Stress the last syllable of *estás* |

💡 **Tip:** Put the 5 words of each day into a flashcard app. By day 30 you have about 130 cards, and a 5-minute daily review keeps them alive. Our [100 easy Spanish words](/100-easy-spanish-words/) list is a good next deck.

## After day 30

You have the basics: about 140 words and phrases, the key verbs, the present tense, a first look at the past, and the phrases you need to ask for help. Here is what to do next:

1. **Keep the daily habit.** Same time, same 20 minutes. Swap the "new words" step for more listening.
2. **Follow a longer plan.** Jump into week 5 of the 12-week plan in [the best way to learn Spanish](/best-way-to-learn-spanish/). It picks up right where this course stops.
3. **Read a little every day.** Start with our [Spanish stories for beginners](/spanish-stories-for-beginners/). Short, simple, and full of the words you just learned.
4. **Have a real conversation.** Book a short tutor lesson or find an exchange partner. Use your day 23 phrases when you get lost.
5. **Redo your day 30 recording every month.** Hearing yourself improve is the best motivation there is.
