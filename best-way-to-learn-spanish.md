# The best way to learn Spanish: method and 12-week plan

The best way to learn Spanish is to hear and read a lot of Spanish you mostly understand, review new words at the right time, and speak early without pressure. Grammar helps, but as a reference, not the main event. Below is the method in plain words, then a 12-week plan you can start today.
{: .lede }

## The method in four ideas

**1. Lots of comprehensible input.** You learn a language by understanding messages in it. "Comprehensible" means you follow most of it, thanks to context, pictures, and slow speech, even if you do not know every word. This is the engine. Everything else supports it. Parrot's page on [comprehensible input](https://www.parrotapp.com/features/comprehensible-input-spanish?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=best-way-to-learn-spanish) shows how the app uses this idea.

**2. Spaced repetition for words.** You forget new words fast unless you see them again. Spaced repetition shows a word again right before you would forget it, so a few minutes a day keeps hundreds of words alive. Our [Spanish flashcards](/spanish-flashcards/) page has a ready deck and a setup guide.

**3. Speak early, but low pressure.** You do not need to wait until you are "ready." Start with short, easy speaking: say a sentence about a video, shadow a speaker, send a voice message. Real conversations come a few weeks later, with a patient tutor or exchange partner.

**4. Grammar as a reference.** Grammar rules help you notice patterns in what you hear. They do not make you fluent on their own. Learn one topic per week, then look for it in your input. Keep a [Spanish grammar](/spanish-grammar/) cheat sheet open for questions.

🎯 **Key point:** Hours of understood Spanish is the number that matters most. If you track one thing, track your listening and reading time.
{: .key }

## The 12-week plan

This plan assumes about 30 minutes a day. "Words" means new words added to your flashcards that week, with the running total in parentheses. If you do 15 minutes a day, halve the words and stretch the plan to 16 weeks or so.

| Week | Focus | Daily listening | Words | Speaking task | Grammar topic | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sounds and first words | 15 min of very easy video with visuals | 70 (70) | Say hello, your name, and where you are from | Alphabet, pronunciation, *soy / eres* | You can introduce yourself in 3 sentences |
| 2 | Everyday things | 15 min | 70 (140) | Name 20 things in your home out loud | *el / la, un / una*, plurals | You follow the gist of a picture-heavy beginner video |
| 3 | Present tense actions | 20 min | 70 (210) | Describe your day in 5 short sentences | Regular -ar, -er, -ir verbs in the present | You can say what you do every day |
| 4 | How things are | 20 min | 70 (280) | Say how you feel and where things are | *estar*, and ser vs estar basics | You understand simple questions about yourself |
| 5 | Wants and plans | 20 min | 70 (350) | Talk about your weekend plans | *ir a* + verb, *querer*, *poder* | You can say what you will do tomorrow |
| 6 | Likes and dislikes | 25 min | 70 (420) | First 15-minute talk with a tutor or partner | *gustar* (me gusta, me gustan) | You survive your first conversation (it counts even if it was messy) |
| 7 | Asking questions | 25 min | 70 (490) | Ask 10 questions in a conversation | Question words, common irregular verbs (*tener, hacer, ir*) | You can keep a conversation going by asking |
| 8 | Daily routine | 25 min | 70 (560) | Describe your morning, step by step | Reflexive verbs (*me levanto, me ducho*) | You understand most of a slow video about daily life |
| 9 | What happened | 30 min | 70 (630) | Tell what you did yesterday | Preterite: regular verbs plus *fui, hice, tuve* | You can tell a short story about yesterday |
| 10 | How things were | 30 min | 70 (700) | Describe a childhood memory | Imperfect, and preterite vs imperfect | You can say "I used to..." and "I was doing..." |
| 11 | Opinions | 30 min | 70 (770) | Give your opinion on a video you watched | *creo que, pienso que*, object pronouns *lo / la* | You can agree, disagree, and say why |
| 12 | Real-life Spanish | 30+ min | 70 (840) | A 20-minute conversation on any topic | *por* vs *para* | You understand most of an easy video with no subtitles |

The past tenses in weeks 9 and 10 trip up most learners. This guide on [preterite vs imperfect](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/preterite-vs-imperfect?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=best-way-to-learn-spanish) has clear examples. Do not wait until you "get it" to move on. It settles with more listening.

Spain or Latin America? Pick the one you hear most, or the one you like. Most of Spain uses *vosotros* and a "th" sound for *z* and soft *c*, and some everyday words differ (*coche* vs *carro*). Speakers from both understand each other fine.

## Daily routines for 15, 30, and 60 minutes

| Activity | 15 minutes | 30 minutes | 60 minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening (comprehensible input) | 8 min | 15 min | 30 min |
| Flashcard review and new words | 5 min | 7 min | 10 min |
| Speaking (shadowing, voice messages, or a call) | 2 min | 5 min | 10 min |
| Grammar or reading | Skip, or weekends only | 3 min | 10 min |

**15 minutes:** watch one short video at your level, review your flashcards, then say two sentences about the video out loud. That is it. A small daily habit beats a big weekend session.

**30 minutes:** two or three videos, flashcards, and a few minutes of shadowing (repeat a sentence right after the speaker, matching their rhythm). Look up one grammar question from your videos.

**60 minutes:** add a longer piece of listening, like a podcast on a walk, and some reading. Replace the speaking block with a 30-minute tutor lesson two or three times a week, and do more listening on the other days.

💡 **Tip:** Stack Spanish on things you already do. Listen while you cook or commute, and review flashcards while you wait for coffee. Habits are easier when they ride on old ones.
{: .tip }

## The fastest realistic way to learn Spanish

The honest answer: speed comes from hours, and from the quality of those hours. There is no app, method, or trick that makes 50 hours feel like 500.

For a sense of scale, the US Foreign Service Institute (FSI) puts Spanish in its easiest group for English speakers. It estimates [about 600 to 750 class hours](https://www.state.gov/foreign-language-training/) (about 24 to 30 weeks of full-time study) to reach professional working proficiency. FSI students study full time with teachers and homework, so this is a fast, intense path, not a casual one.

Here is what that means for normal schedules, using about 600 hours as the target:

| Daily time | About how long to 600 hours |
|---|---|
| 30 minutes | about 3.3 years |
| 1 hour | about 1.6 years |
| 2 hours | about 10 months |
| 4 hours | about 5 months |

You will not need 600 hours to enjoy Spanish. Simple conversations, travel, and easy shows come much earlier. To go faster:

- **Add hours, not apps.** Turn dead time (commute, chores, the gym) into listening time.
- **Talk to people every week.** Two or three tutor lessons a week make your listening usable in real life.
- **Choose content you enjoy.** You will watch more of it, and more is the whole game.
- **Go somewhere Spanish-speaking, if you can.** A few weeks of immersion works best after you can already follow slow speech.

## Learning Spanish as an adult

Adults often think they missed the window. You did not. Kids may end up with a more native accent, but adults have real advantages: you can read, you can plan, you can use tools like flashcards, and you understand grammar explanations in one go.

What changes for adults is time and pressure. You have a job, a family, and a fear of sounding silly. So:

- **Plan small.** Fifteen minutes on a busy day keeps the habit alive.
- **Lower the stakes.** Practice speaking with an AI tutor, a voice message, or a patient tutor before you try it at a party.
- **Measure the right thing.** Count hours of Spanish you understood, not lessons finished.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Only doing app drills | You learn words but cannot follow real speech | Make listening at least half of your time |
| Content that is too hard | You understand little, so you learn little | Pick content where you follow most of it, then move up |
| Studying grammar first, for months | Rules without input do not become speech | One grammar topic a week, then look for it in your listening |
| Waiting to speak until "ready" | Speaking feels scarier every month | Start with shadowing and voice messages in week 1 |
| Adding too many flashcards | Reviews pile up and you quit | Keep to about 10 new words a day, fewer if reviews take over 15 minutes |
| Switching methods every few weeks | You never give one approach enough hours | Commit to one plan for 12 weeks |
| Hearing only one speaker or accent | Other voices feel impossible later | Pick a main accent, then add speakers from other countries from about week 8 |

⚠️ **Watch out:** Feeling slow in weeks 2 to 5 is normal. Your listening improves before you notice it. Do not judge the method until week 12.
