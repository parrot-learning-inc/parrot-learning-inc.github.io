# Best app to learn Spanish in 2026: 13 apps ranked

The best app to learn Spanish gives you lots of real Spanish you can understand, a way to keep new words, and a way to speak. We rank Parrot first for that mix, with Dreaming Spanish and italki close behind. Full disclosure: we make Parrot, so we show our scoring method and name what every other app does well.

## How we scored the apps

We gave every app a score from 1 to 10 on six criteria, then took the weighted average. These scores are our judgment from using the apps as learners, not a lab test. Your own score may differ if your goal is different.

| Criterion | Weight | What we look at |
|---|---|---|
| Real Spanish input | 25% | How much Spanish from native speakers you hear and read, at a level you can follow |
| Speaking practice | 20% | How often you actually produce Spanish out loud, with some feedback |
| Word review | 15% | Whether new words come back at the right time so you keep them |
| Fit for your level | 15% | Whether the app adapts to you, from first day to intermediate |
| Value | 15% | What you get for free, and what you get for the price |
| Easy to keep using | 10% | How easy it is to open the app every day and not quit in week three |

🎯 **Key point:** Input gets the biggest weight on purpose. You learn a language mostly by understanding a lot of it. Apps that keep you tapping on single words score lower, even if they feel productive.

## The 13 apps, one by one

### 1. Parrot (9.2)

Parrot teaches Spanish with short videos from native speakers, matched to your level, and the feed follows your interests. So you spend your time on real Spanish, not on translating "the boy eats an apple." Tap any word in a video to see its meaning and save it. Saved words become flashcards, so review happens on its own. There is practice for listening, reading, and speaking, plus an AI Spanish tutor for low-pressure conversation. It works on iPhone, Android, and the web, and it is free to start.

**Honest cons:** it is built around video, so it asks for some screen time with the sound on. If you want a strict textbook path through grammar, keep a grammar reference next to it. An AI tutor is good practice, but it is not a human teacher.

### 2. Dreaming Spanish (8.6)

Dreaming Spanish is pure comprehensible input: thousands of videos where native speakers talk slowly and clearly, sorted from "superbeginner" up. You hear a mix of accents from Spain and Latin America, and the app tracks your listening hours. The free library alone is enough for months. **Cons:** there is almost no grammar, speaking, or word review by design. The early weeks can feel slow if you like structure.

### 3. italki (8.4)

italki is a marketplace for one-on-one video lessons with tutors. You can pick a tutor by country, price, and style, so you can choose Mexican, Spanish, or Argentine Spanish. Nothing beats a real person for speaking practice. **Cons:** you pay for every lesson, quality depends on the tutor, and you need to schedule. It also does not give you study material between lessons.

### 4. Language Transfer (8.2)

Language Transfer's Complete Spanish is a free audio course where a teacher guides a student to build sentences by thinking, not memorizing. It is one of the fastest ways to see how Spanish sentences fit together. **Cons:** it is audio only, and you mostly hear the student, who is a learner, not a native speaker. There is no word review and little listening practice at natural speed.

### 5. Pimsleur (7.9)

Pimsleur gives you 30-minute audio lessons that make you answer out loud from lesson one. It is great for pronunciation and for commutes. **Cons:** you learn few words per lesson, the price is high for the amount of content, and there is little reading. See our [Pimsleur alternative](https://www.parrotapp.com/alternatives/pimsleur-alternative?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=best-app-to-learn-spanish) page for a side-by-side.

### 6. Babbel (7.7)

Babbel has short, well-built lessons with grammar explained in English and practical dialogues. It is a good choice if you want someone to tell you what to learn next. **Cons:** the path is fixed, most listening is scripted and slow, and speaking exercises are mostly short answers. Compare it on our [Babbel alternative](https://www.parrotapp.com/alternatives/babbel-alternative?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=best-app-to-learn-spanish) page.

### 7. SpanishDict (7.6)

SpanishDict is the reference most learners keep open: dictionary, full verb conjugation tables, example sentences, and clear grammar guides. It is free. **Cons:** it is a tool to look things up, not a course. The free version shows ads.

### 8. Anki (7.5)

Anki is a free flashcard app with spaced repetition. It shows each card right before you would forget it. You control everything: card format, audio, images, daily limits. It is free on desktop, Android, and the web; the iOS app is a one-time purchase. **Cons:** setup takes time, it has no Spanish of its own, and it is easy to add too many cards and burn out. Our [Spanish flashcards](/spanish-flashcards/) page has a ready deck.

### 9. Duolingo (7.3)

Duolingo is the easiest way to start: it is free, fun, and the streaks really help you build a daily habit. **Cons:** sentences are short and often odd, and you hear little real Spanish at natural speed. It can feel like progress while your listening stays weak. If that sounds familiar, our [Duolingo alternative](https://www.parrotapp.com/alternatives/duolingo-alternative?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=best-app-to-learn-spanish) page explains what to add.

### 10. Busuu (7.1)

Busuu follows a structured course by level and lets native speakers in the community correct your written and spoken answers. That feedback is a real plus. **Cons:** the free tier is limited, and the quality of community feedback varies.

### 11. Lingopie (6.9)

Lingopie lets you watch Spanish TV shows and movies with two sets of clickable subtitles, then review words as flashcards. **Cons:** the shows are made for native speakers, so they are hard for beginners. It works best once you can follow simple conversations.

### 12. Memrise (6.6)

Memrise mixes vocabulary review with short videos of local people saying useful phrases, which is good for your ear. **Cons:** the app changed a lot in recent years, lessons can feel repetitive, and there is not much longer listening.

### 13. Rosetta Stone (6.4)

Rosetta Stone teaches with pictures and no English, and its speech recognition is good for pronunciation. **Cons:** with no translations, abstract words get confusing, progress is slow, and the content does not feel like real Spanish conversation. ## Pick by your goal

| Your goal | Start with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Understand real Spanish (shows, people, podcasts) | Parrot or Dreaming Spanish | Hours of listening you can follow is what builds understanding |
| Speak with confidence | italki, plus Parrot's speaking practice | You need real conversations, and low-pressure practice between them |
| Spend nothing | Language Transfer + Dreaming Spanish free videos | One builds sentences, one builds listening |

| Pass a class or exam | Babbel + SpanishDict | Clear structure, plus a grammar reference |

## How to combine two apps

The best setup is usually two apps: one for **input** (listening and reading) and one for **output or review** (speaking, writing, or flashcards). Each app does one job well, and you do not waste time on two apps that do the same thing.

| Combo | Input app | Second app | A simple week |
|---|---|---|---|
| The all-rounder | Parrot | italki | Parrot 20 min a day, one 30-minute tutor lesson a week |
| The free one | Dreaming Spanish | Language Transfer | 20 min of video a day, one audio lesson every other day |
| The word builder | Dreaming Spanish | Anki | 30 min of video, 10 min of flashcards from words you heard |
| The structured one | Babbel | Parrot | One Babbel lesson, then 15 min of real videos on the same topic |
| The commuter | Pimsleur | Parrot | Pimsleur in the car, Parrot videos at lunch |

💡 **Tip:** Look up grammar in SpanishDict only when a question comes up in your input app. A two-minute answer to your own question sticks better than a lesson you did not ask for.
{: .tip }

⚠️ **Watch out:** Three or more apps usually means 5 minutes in each and progress in none. If you add a new app, drop one.
