# Free Spanish flashcards: the 300 most common words

These free Spanish flashcards cover 300 of the most common Spanish words, in frequency order, split into three decks of 100. Each card has the English meaning, the part of speech, and a short example sentence. Download the deck for Anki or Quizlet below, and learn about 10 cards a day.
{: .lede }

A small number of words make up a big share of everyday Spanish. Words like **de**, **que**, **ser**, **tener**, and **ir** show up in almost every sentence. Learn them first, and everything you read or hear gets easier.

**How we picked the words:** we ranked them by our best judgment of how often learners will meet them, based on common frequency patterns in spoken and written Spanish. It is not a copy of one official corpus. Verbs are in the infinitive (tener, not tengo) and adjectives in the masculine form (bueno). Nouns show their gender: noun (m) or noun (f).

## How to import the deck into Anki

Anki is a free flashcard program that shows each card again right before you are likely to forget it. Get it from [ankiweb.net](https://ankiweb.net/), which also syncs your decks to your phone.

1. **Download the file.** Right-click [spanish-flashcards-anki.txt](/data/spanish-flashcards-anki.txt) and choose "Save link as".
2. **Open Anki on your computer.** Import works best in the desktop app.
3. **Go to File > Import** and choose the .txt file you saved.
4. **Check the settings.** The file tells Anki to use tabs as the separator and plain text, not HTML. Recent versions of Anki read these settings from the file.
5. **Pick the note type and deck.** Choose **Basic** as the note type. Pick an existing deck or create one, like "Spanish Top 300".
6. **Check the field mapping.** Column 1 should go to **Front** (Spanish), column 2 to **Back** (English and example), and column 3 to **Tags**.
7. **Click Import.** You now have 300 cards.

Every card has two tags: `parrot_top300` and its deck, like `parrot_top300_deck1`. To study one deck at a time, search for `tag:parrot_top300_deck1` in the Anki browser, or make a filtered deck with that search. To sync to your phone, log in to AnkiWeb in the desktop app and sync, then sync on the phone app.

💡 **Tip:** Want cards in both directions (Spanish to English and English to Spanish)? Choose **Basic (and reversed card)** in step 5. Start with Spanish on the front, though. Recognizing a word is easier than producing it.

## How to import the deck into Quizlet

Quizlet wants a tab between the term and the definition, and a new line between cards. A spreadsheet gives you exactly that when you copy cells.

1. Open [the CSV](/data/spanish-flashcards.csv) in Google Sheets or Excel.
2. Select the **spanish** and **english** columns for the rows you want (for example, ranks 1-100 for deck 1). Copy them.
3. In Quizlet, create a new flashcard set and click **Import**.
4. Paste. Set "Between term and definition" to **Tab** and "Between cards" to **New line**.
5. Check the preview, then import and save the set.

Quizlet changes its menus from time to time, so the button names may be a little different. The tab and new line settings stay the same.

## How to make good flashcards

Our deck is a starting point. The best cards are often the ones you make yourself, from Spanish you actually met. A few rules:

- **One idea per card.** One word or one short chunk. Not a whole verb table.
- **Add a sentence.** "Tengo hambre" teaches you more than "tener = to have".
- **Learn nouns with el or la.** Learn **la mesa**, not mesa. Gender is much easier to learn at the start than to fix later.
- **Make it personal.** "Mi hermana vive en Austin" beats any sentence from a book, because it is about your life.
- **Delete cards you keep failing.** If a card fails again and again, rewrite it with a better example or drop it. Some words are easier to learn later.

## A daily review routine (about 10 minutes)

1. **Do your reviews first.** These are the cards Anki thinks you are about to forget.
2. **Then add 10 new cards.** In Anki, set this under the deck options (New cards/day). Ten a day finishes one deck in 10 days and all 300 words in about a month.
3. **Say every answer out loud** before you flip the card. It trains your mouth, not only your eyes.
4. **Be honest.** If you hesitated a lot, press "Hard" or "Again". Lying to Anki only hurts you.
5. **Keep it to about 10 minutes.** Daily reviews grow for the first few weeks. If they take more than 15 minutes, drop to 5 new cards a day until things calm down.

⚠️ **Watch out:** Missing a few days is fine. Missing a week and coming back to 400 reviews is how a lot of people quit. Do a short session every day, even if it is just your reviews.

## Why flashcards alone are not enough

Flashcards are great at one job: helping you remember words. But knowing that **quedar** means "to stay" does not mean you understand "¿Quedamos a las ocho?" when someone says it fast. For that, you need lots of Spanish you can mostly understand. Language teachers call this comprehensible input.

The best mix is simple: flashcards for 10 minutes, then real Spanish at your level for as long as you enjoy it. Easy videos, [Spanish stories for beginners](/spanish-stories-for-beginners/), podcasts, or shows with Spanish subtitles all count.
