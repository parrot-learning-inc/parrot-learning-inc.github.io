# Easy phrases in Spanish for real-life situations

These 100 easy phrases in Spanish cover the moments you actually face: saying hi, being polite, meeting people, not understanding, ordering food, shopping, getting around, checking in, reacting, and getting help. Each phrase has a pronunciation guide and a short note on when to use it. Learn the first 20 and you can handle a surprising amount.
{: .lede }

Phrases are the fastest way to start speaking, because you do not need grammar to use them. You say "la cuenta, por favor", and the check shows up. Grammar can come later.

[Download the CSV](/data/easy-phrases-in-spanish.csv) with every phrase on this page, pronunciation, situation, and notes.

The "say it like" column uses English sounds, with the stressed syllable in capitals. The hints follow Latin American Spanish. In most of Spain, z and c before e or i sound like "th". Notes tell you when a phrase is casual (tú) or polite (usted), and when Spain and Latin America say it differently.

## Tú or usted?

Spanish has two ways to say "you". **Tú** is casual: friends, family, kids, and people your age. **Usted** is polite: older people, officials, and staff in formal places. Many phrases on this page come in both versions, and the notes show you which is which.

The rule of thumb is simple. Start with usted when you talk to a stranger who is older than you or who is serving you in a formal place. If they answer with tú, switch to tú. In Spain, people use tú very quickly, even with strangers. In many parts of Latin America, usted stays longer. In Argentina and Uruguay you will also hear **vos** instead of tú, but people there understand tú fine.

## Greetings

In Spanish, you greet people almost everywhere: in shops, in elevators, at the doctor's office. A quick "buenos días" or "buenas" when you walk in is normal and friendly. Skipping it can seem a little cold.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buenos días.** | Good morning. | BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs | Use until about lunchtime. Fine with anyone. |
| **Buenas tardes.** | Good afternoon. | BWEH-nahs TAHR-dehs | From lunch until it gets dark. |
| **Buenas noches.** | Good evening. / Good night. | BWEH-nahs NOH-chehs | Works as hello and as goodbye after dark. |
| **¿Qué tal?** | How's it going? | keh TAHL | Casual and friendly. Works everywhere. |
| **¿Cómo estás?** | How are you? | KOH-moh ehs-TAHS | Casual (tú). For friends and people your age. |
| **¿Cómo está usted?** | How are you? | KOH-moh ehs-TAH oos-TEHD | Polite (usted). For older people and officials. |
| **Muy bien, ¿y tú?** | Very well, and you? | MWEE BYEHN ee TOO | Casual reply. Polite: ¿y usted? |
| **Hasta luego.** | See you later. | AHS-tah LWEH-goh | Common goodbye, even with strangers in shops. |
| **Hasta mañana.** | See you tomorrow. | AHS-tah mah-NYAH-nah | For people you will see the next day. |
| **Nos vemos.** | See you. | nohs BEH-mohs | Casual goodbye. |

## Polite basics

Spanish speakers use **por favor** and **gracias** a lot, but a simple "Un café, por favor" is not rude at all. Tone matters more than extra words. For more ways to thank people, from casual to formal, see Parrot's guide on [how to say thank you in Spanish](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/how-to-say-thank-you-in-spanish?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=easy-phrases-in-spanish).

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Por favor.** | Please. | pohr fah-BOHR | Add it to any request. |
| **Gracias.** | Thank you. | GRAH-syahs | Works everywhere, all day. |
| **Muchas gracias.** | Thank you very much. | MOO-chahs GRAH-syahs | A warmer thank you. |
| **De nada.** | You're welcome. | deh NAH-dah | In Mexico you also hear "Con gusto" or "Por nada". |
| **Perdón.** | Sorry. / Excuse me. | pehr-DOHN | For small mistakes, or to get attention. |
| **Con permiso.** | Excuse me (coming through). | kohn pehr-MEE-soh | When you pass someone or leave the table. |
| **Lo siento.** | I'm sorry. | loh SYEHN-toh | A real apology, or sympathy for bad news. |
| **Disculpe.** | Excuse me. | dees-KOOL-peh | Polite way to get a stranger's attention. Casual: disculpa. |
| **No pasa nada.** | No problem. / It's fine. | noh PAH-sah NAH-dah | Very common in Spain. Understood everywhere. |
| **Sí, claro.** | Yes, of course. | SEE KLAH-roh | Friendly yes. |

## Meeting people

When you meet someone new, the first three questions are almost always the same: your name, where you are from, and what you do. Learn the answers to these three, and your first conversations get much easier.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **¿Cómo te llamas?** | What's your name? | KOH-moh teh YAH-mahs | Casual. Polite: ¿Cómo se llama? |
| **Me llamo...** | My name is... | meh YAH-moh | The most common way to say your name. |
| **Mucho gusto.** | Nice to meet you. | MOO-choh GOOS-toh | Works everywhere. |
| **Encantado. / Encantada.** | Pleased to meet you. | ehn-kahn-TAH-doh / ehn-kahn-TAH-dah | Say encantada if you are a woman. |
| **¿De dónde eres?** | Where are you from? | deh DOHN-deh EH-rehs | Casual. Polite: ¿De dónde es usted? |
| **Soy de...** | I'm from... | SOY deh | Soy de Canadá, soy de Texas, and so on. |
| **¿A qué te dedicas?** | What do you do (for work)? | ah KEH teh deh-DEE-kahs | Casual. Sounds more natural than ¿Cuál es tu trabajo? |
| **Estoy aprendiendo español.** | I'm learning Spanish. | ehs-TOY ah-prehn-DYEHN-doh ehs-pah-NYOHL | People usually slow down when they hear this. |
| **¿Hablas inglés?** | Do you speak English? | AH-blahs een-GLEHS | Casual. Polite: ¿Habla inglés? |
| **Este es mi amigo.** | This is my friend. | EHS-teh ehs mee ah-MEE-goh | For a woman: Esta es mi amiga. |

## When you do not understand

This is the most important group on the page. You will not understand everything at first, and that is fine. These phrases keep the conversation going instead of switching to English.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No entiendo.** | I don't understand. | noh ehn-TYEHN-doh | Add "lo siento" to sound softer. |
| **¿Puede repetir, por favor?** | Can you repeat that, please? | PWEH-deh reh-peh-TEER pohr fah-BOHR | Polite. Casual: ¿Puedes repetir? |
| **Más despacio, por favor.** | Slower, please. | MAHS dehs-PAH-syoh pohr fah-BOHR | Nobody minds. Use it a lot. |
| **¿Qué significa...?** | What does... mean? | keh seeg-nee-FEE-kah | Point at the word or say it. |
| **¿Cómo se dice... en español?** | How do you say... in Spanish? | KOH-moh seh DEE-seh... ehn ehs-pah-NYOHL | Your best tool to learn new words. |
| **¿Cómo?** | Sorry? / What? | KOH-moh | A polite way to say "what?". In Mexico you also hear ¿Mande? |
| **Hablo un poco de español.** | I speak a little Spanish. | AH-bloh oon POH-koh deh ehs-pah-NYOHL | Sets the speed of the chat. |
| **¿Me lo puede escribir?** | Can you write it down for me? | meh loh PWEH-deh ehs-kree-BEER | Polite. Good for addresses and prices. |
| **No sé.** | I don't know. | noh SEH | Short and normal, not rude. |
| **Un momento, por favor.** | One moment, please. | oon moh-MEHN-toh pohr fah-BOHR | Buys you time to think. |

🎯 **Key point:** "Más despacio, por favor" is your best friend. Native speakers talk fast, but almost everyone slows down happily when you ask.

## Restaurant and café

Ordering food is where most people use Spanish first. The good news: the waiter knows the script too. You can mix and match these phrases with any dish or drink on the menu.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Una mesa para dos, por favor.** | A table for two, please. | OO-nah MEH-sah PAH-rah DOHS pohr fah-BOHR | Change the number as needed. |
| **¿Me trae la carta, por favor?** | Can you bring me the menu, please? | meh TRAH-eh lah KAHR-tah pohr fah-BOHR | "La carta" is common in Spain. "El menú" is common in Latin America. |
| **Quisiera un café.** | I'd like a coffee. | kee-SYEH-rah oon kah-FEH | Softer than "quiero". |
| **Para mí, el pollo.** | For me, the chicken. | PAH-rah MEE ehl POH-yoh | Easy way to order. Change the dish. |
| **¿Qué me recomienda?** | What do you recommend? | keh meh reh-koh-MYEHN-dah | Polite. Waiters like this question. |
| **Sin hielo, por favor.** | No ice, please. | seen YEH-loh pohr fah-BOHR | Hielo = ice. |
| **Está muy rico.** | It's delicious. | ehs-TAH MWEE REE-koh | Rico means tasty when you talk about food. |
| **La cuenta, por favor.** | The check, please. | lah KWEHN-tah pohr fah-BOHR | In many places the waiter waits for you to ask. |
| **¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?** | Can I pay by card? | PWEH-doh pah-GAHR kohn tahr-HEH-tah | Small places sometimes take cash only. |
| **¿Dónde está el baño?** | Where is the bathroom? | DOHN-deh ehs-TAH ehl BAH-nyoh | In Spain you also hear "el servicio" or "el aseo". |

## Shopping

Prices are the hardest part of shopping in a new language, because numbers come fast. If you do not catch the price, ask "¿Me lo puede escribir?" from the section above, or look at the screen on the register.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **¿Cuánto cuesta?** | How much does it cost? | KWAHN-toh KWEHS-tah | For more than one thing: ¿Cuánto cuestan? |
| **¿Cuánto es?** | How much is it (in total)? | KWAHN-toh EHS | Use it at the register. |
| **Solo estoy mirando.** | I'm just looking. | SOH-loh ehs-TOY mee-RAHN-doh | Polite way to say no to help. |
| **¿Tiene esto en otra talla?** | Do you have this in another size? | TYEH-neh EHS-toh ehn OH-trah TAH-yah | Talla is for clothes. For shoes: número. |
| **Me lo llevo.** | I'll take it. | meh loh YEH-boh | Use "me la llevo" for a feminine noun, like la camisa. |
| **Es muy caro.** | It's very expensive. | EHS MWEE KAH-roh | Say it with a smile at markets. |
| **¿Me hace un descuento?** | Can you give me a discount? | meh AH-seh oon dehs-KWEHN-toh | Fine at markets. Strange in chain stores. |
| **¿Aceptan tarjeta?** | Do you take cards? | ah-SEHP-tahn tahr-HEH-tah | Ask before you order or pick things up. |
| **Una bolsa, por favor.** | A bag, please. | OO-nah BOHL-sah pohr fah-BOHR | Some stores charge for bags. |
| **¿A qué hora abren?** | What time do you open? | ah KEH OH-rah AH-brehn | Closing time: ¿A qué hora cierran? |

## Directions and transport

Asking for directions is easy. Understanding the answer is the hard part. Listen for three words: **derecha** (right), **izquierda** (left), and **recto** or **derecho** (straight). Then say "gracias" and follow the hand that points.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **¿Dónde está...?** | Where is...? | DOHN-deh ehs-TAH | Add any place: el metro, el museo, la playa. |
| **¿Cómo llego a...?** | How do I get to...? | KOH-moh YEH-goh ah | For a place name: ¿Cómo llego al centro? |
| **¿Está lejos?** | Is it far? | ehs-TAH LEH-hohs | Answer might be "está cerca" (it's close). |
| **A la derecha.** | To the right. | ah lah deh-REH-chah | Do not mix it up with "derecho" (straight). |
| **A la izquierda.** | To the left. | ah lah ees-KYEHR-dah | Izquierda is the hardest word here. Practice it. |
| **Todo recto.** | Straight ahead. | TOH-doh REHK-toh | Spain. In Latin America you hear "todo derecho" or just "derecho". |
| **Un boleto para..., por favor.** | A ticket to..., please. | oon boh-LEH-toh PAH-rah... pohr fah-BOHR | Boleto in Latin America. In Spain: un billete. |
| **¿A qué hora sale el tren?** | What time does the train leave? | ah KEH OH-rah SAH-leh ehl TREHN | Works for el autobús and el vuelo too. |
| **¿Me lleva a esta dirección?** | Can you take me to this address? | meh YEH-bah ah EHS-tah dee-rehk-SYOHN | Polite. For a taxi driver. Show the address. |
| **¿Dónde está la parada de autobús?** | Where is the bus stop? | DOHN-deh ehs-TAH lah pah-RAH-dah deh ow-toh-BOOS | The bus is "el camión" in Mexico and "la guagua" in the Caribbean and the Canary Islands. |

## Hotel

Staff at big hotels often speak English, but small hotels and guesthouses may not. These phrases cover check-in, check-out, and the usual small problems.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tengo una reserva.** | I have a reservation. | TEHN-goh OO-nah reh-SEHR-bah | In Latin America you also hear "una reservación". |
| **A nombre de...** | Under the name... | ah NOHM-breh deh | Then say your last name. |
| **¿Tiene habitaciones libres?** | Do you have any rooms available? | TYEH-neh ah-bee-tah-SYOH-nehs LEE-brehs | For walk-ins. |
| **¿Está incluido el desayuno?** | Is breakfast included? | ehs-TAH een-kloo-EE-doh ehl deh-sah-YOO-noh | Desayuno = breakfast. |
| **¿A qué hora es la salida?** | What time is check-out? | ah KEH OH-rah EHS lah sah-LEE-dah | Many hotels also just say "el check-out". |
| **¿Cuál es la contraseña del wifi?** | What's the wifi password? | KWAHL EHS lah kohn-trah-SEH-nyah dehl WEE-fee | The way people say "wifi" changes by country. |
| **No funciona el aire acondicionado.** | The air conditioning doesn't work. | noh foon-SYOH-nah ehl AY-reh ah-kohn-dee-syoh-NAH-doh | Swap in la ducha (shower) or la luz (light). |
| **¿Puedo dejar las maletas aquí?** | Can I leave my bags here? | PWEH-doh deh-HAHR lahs mah-LEH-tahs ah-KEE | Useful before check-in or after check-out. |
| **La llave, por favor.** | The key, please. | lah YAH-beh pohr fah-BOHR | Key cards are often "la tarjeta". |
| **Necesito más toallas.** | I need more towels. | neh-seh-SEE-toh MAHS toh-AH-yahs | Swap in almohadas (pillows) or papel higiénico (toilet paper). |

## Feelings and reactions

Short reactions keep a conversation alive, even when you cannot say much. A well-timed "¡qué bien!" or "¡no me digas!" shows that you follow along. Notice that Spanish uses **tener** for hunger and thirst, not "to be".

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **¡Qué bien!** | Great! / How nice! | keh BYEHN | The most useful happy reaction. |
| **¡Qué pena!** | What a shame! | keh PEH-nah | In Colombia it can also mean "how embarrassing" or "sorry". |
| **¡Genial!** | Great! | heh-NYAHL | Casual and positive. |
| **Estoy cansado. / Estoy cansada.** | I'm tired. | ehs-TOY kahn-SAH-doh / kahn-SAH-dah | Say cansada if you are a woman. |
| **Tengo hambre.** | I'm hungry. | TEHN-goh AHM-breh | Literally: I have hunger. |
| **Tengo sed.** | I'm thirsty. | TEHN-goh SEHD | Literally: I have thirst. |
| **Me encanta.** | I love it. | meh ehn-KAHN-tah | For things and places, not people. |
| **No me gusta.** | I don't like it. | noh meh GOOS-tah | For more than one thing: No me gustan. |
| **Me da igual.** | I don't mind. | meh dah ee-GWAHL | Can sound cold. "Como quieras" (as you like) is softer. |
| **¡No me digas!** | No way! / You don't say! | noh meh DEE-gahs | For surprising news. |

## Emergencies

In Spain and the rest of the European Union, the emergency number is 112. In Mexico and the United States, it is 911. Other countries use other numbers, so check before you travel.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **¡Ayuda!** | Help! | ah-YOO-dah | You also hear ¡Socorro! (soh-KOH-rroh). |
| **Necesito un médico.** | I need a doctor. | neh-seh-SEE-toh oon MEH-dee-koh | Say it clearly and early. |
| **Llame a una ambulancia.** | Call an ambulance. | YAH-meh ah OO-nah ahm-boo-LAHN-syah | Polite command to a stranger. |
| **Llame a la policía.** | Call the police. | YAH-meh ah lah poh-lee-SEE-ah | Polite command to a stranger. |
| **Me robaron la cartera.** | My wallet was stolen. | meh roh-BAH-rohn lah kahr-TEH-rah | Literally: they stole my wallet. In Spain: Me han robado la cartera. |
| **Estoy perdido. / Estoy perdida.** | I'm lost. | ehs-TOY pehr-DEE-doh / pehr-DEE-dah | Say perdida if you are a woman. |
| **Soy alérgico a... / Soy alérgica a...** | I'm allergic to... | SOY ah-LEHR-hee-koh ah | Alérgica if you are a woman. Example: Soy alérgico a los cacahuetes (peanuts; maní or cacahuates in Latin America). |
| **¿Dónde está el hospital?** | Where is the hospital? | DOHN-deh ehs-TAH ehl ohs-pee-TAHL | The h is silent. |
| **¿Dónde está la farmacia?** | Where is the pharmacy? | DOHN-deh ehs-TAH lah fahr-MAH-syah | For small problems, start here. |
| **Me duele aquí.** | It hurts here. | meh DWEH-leh ah-KEE | Point to the place. |

## 10 phrases that make you sound natural

The phrases above are correct everywhere. These ones are what people actually say among friends. Most are regional, so use them where they belong. Say "¡vale!" in Mexico City and people will know where you learned your Spanish.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **¿Qué onda?** | What's up? | keh OHN-dah | Mexico and parts of Central America. Very casual. |
| **¡Vale!** | OK! / Sure! | BAH-leh | Spain. You hear it all day. |
| **Dale.** | OK, let's do it. | DAH-leh | Argentina, Uruguay, the Caribbean, and more. Casual. |
| **¿En serio?** | Seriously? / Really? | ehn SEH-ryoh | Works everywhere. |
| **Ni idea.** | No idea. | nee ee-DEH-ah | Casual. |
| **¡Buen provecho!** | Enjoy your meal! | BWEHN proh-BEH-choh | Say it to people who are eating. In Mexico people often say "provecho" to strangers as they walk past a table. |
| **Igualmente.** | Same to you. / Likewise. | ee-gwahl-MEHN-teh | Reply to "mucho gusto" or "buen fin de semana". |
| **Pues...** | Well... | PWEHS | Filler word while you think. Very common. |
| **¡Qué padre!** | How cool! | keh PAH-dreh | Mexico. In Spain: ¡Qué guay! |
| **Ahorita.** | In a moment. / Right now. | ah-oh-REE-tah | Mexico and parts of Latin America. It can mean now, soon, or much later. |

💡 **Tip:** Pick the natural phrases from the country you care about most. If you are going to Mexico, learn ¿qué onda?, ahorita, and ¡qué padre! If you are going to Spain, learn ¡vale! first.

## Phrases to avoid

Some phrases look like English but mean something else. These are the classic beginner mistakes. Some are just funny. A couple can make a room go very quiet.

| Avoid | What it can mean | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| ~~Estoy caliente.~~ | I'm horny (in many places), or your body is hot to the touch. | **Tengo calor. (I'm hot.)** |
| ~~Estoy embarazada.~~ | I'm pregnant. | **Qué vergüenza. / Me da vergüenza. (I'm embarrassed.)** |
| ~~Estoy excitado.~~ | Usually sexual. | **Estoy emocionado. (I'm excited.)** |
| ~~Estoy constipado.~~ | I have a cold (in Spain). | **Estoy estreñido. (I'm constipated.)** |
| ~~Soy 25 años.~~ | Nothing. It's not Spanish. | **Tengo 25 años. (I'm 25.)** |
| ~~Te introduzco a mi amigo.~~ | Something very strange. Introducir means to put inside. | **Te presento a mi amigo. (Let me introduce my friend.)** |
| ~~¿Dónde puedo coger un taxi?~~ | Normal in Spain, but coger is vulgar in Mexico, Argentina, and much of Latin America. | **¿Dónde puedo tomar un taxi?** |

## How to learn these phrases

1. **Start with your next real situation.** Going to a café this week? Learn that group first.
2. **Learn phrases as one chunk.** Do not take "¿Dónde está el baño?" apart word by word. Say it as one piece until it comes out on its own.
3. **Say them out loud.** Reading silently does not train your mouth. Parrot's [Spanish speaking practice](https://www.parrotapp.com/features/spanish-speaking-practice?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=easy-phrases-in-spanish) lets you practice out loud before you need it for real.
4. **Swap one word.** Once "Quisiera un café" is easy, try "Quisiera un té", then "Quisiera la cuenta". One phrase becomes ten.
