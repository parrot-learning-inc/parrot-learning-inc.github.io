# Spanish travel guide Mexico: phrases, tacos, and tips

This Spanish travel guide for Mexico gives you the phrases you will actually use, from the airport to the taco stand, plus rough prices, tipping rules, slang, and the manners that make people smile at you. Learn the bold phrases first, and you can handle most situations with a smile and a little patience.
{: .lede }

Everything here uses Mexican Spanish. A few words differ from Spain: in Mexico a city bus is a **camión**, a cell phone is a **celular**, and a reservation is a **reservación**.

## The 3 phrases that do most of the work

| Spanish | English | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Me da..., por favor.** | Can I have..., please? | meh DAH... por fah-VOR |
| **¿Cuánto cuesta?** | How much is it? | KWAN-toh KWES-tah |
| **¿Mande?** | Sorry? Pardon? (polite, very Mexican) | MAHN-deh |

"Me da" sounds much more natural than "quiero" (I want). Add "por favor" and "gracias" to everything.

## Survival phrases by situation

### Airport and immigration

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Vengo de vacaciones. | I am here on vacation. |
| Me quedo dos semanas. | I am staying two weeks. |
| Me hospedo en el hotel... | I am staying at the ... hotel. |
| No tengo nada que declarar. | I have nothing to declare. |
| ¿Dónde recojo mi equipaje? | Where do I pick up my luggage? |
| ¿Dónde hay un cajero automático? | Where is there an ATM? |

### Taxi, Uber, and DiDi

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Me lleva a esta dirección, por favor? | Can you take me to this address, please? |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta al centro? | How much is it to downtown? |
| ¿A nombre de quién? | Under what name? (drivers ask to confirm your ride) |
| A nombre de Sam. | Under the name Sam. |
| Estoy en la puerta tres. | I am at door three. |
| Aquí está bien, gracias. | Here is fine, thanks. |

At big airports, use the official taxi booths inside the terminal (look for "taxi autorizado") or a ride app. Pay at the booth, then show your ticket to the driver.

### Metro and bus

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Dónde compro la tarjeta? | Where do I buy the card? |
| ¿Qué línea va a...? | Which line goes to...? |
| ¿Este camión va al centro? | Does this bus go downtown? |
| ¿Dónde me bajo para...? | Where do I get off for...? |
| Con permiso. | Excuse me. (to get past people) |
| ¡Bajan! | Getting off! (what people shout at the door) |

### Hotel

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo una reservación a nombre de... | I have a reservation under the name... |
| ¿El desayuno está incluido? | Is breakfast included? |
| ¿A qué hora tengo que dejar el cuarto? | What time is checkout? |
| ¿Cuál es la contraseña del wifi? | What is the wifi password? |
| No hay agua caliente. | There is no hot water. |
| ¿Me puede guardar la maleta? | Can you keep my suitcase for me? |

### Restaurant

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Una mesa para dos, por favor. | A table for two, please. |
| ¿Qué me recomienda? | What do you recommend? |
| ¿Pica? | Is it spicy? |
| Sin chile, por favor. / Poquito picante. | No chili, please. / Just a little spicy. |
| ¿Aceptan tarjeta? | Do you take cards? |
| La cuenta, por favor. | The check, please. |

### Market and bargaining

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cuánto cuesta este? | How much is this one? |
| Solo estoy viendo, gracias. | I am just looking, thanks. |
| ¿Me lo deja en doscientos? | Would you let me have it for two hundred? |
| ¿Me hace un descuento si me llevo dos? | Will you give me a discount if I take two? |
| Me lo llevo. | I will take it. |
| ¿Tiene cambio? | Do you have change? |

Bargain gently at craft and souvenir markets. Do not bargain at food stalls, shops with price tags, or for things that already cost a few pesos.

### Pharmacy and health

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me duele el estómago / la cabeza. | My stomach / head hurts. |
| Tengo fiebre / diarrea. | I have a fever / diarrhea. |
| ¿Tiene algo para...? | Do you have something for...? |
| Soy alérgico (alérgica) a la penicilina. | I am allergic to penicillin. |
| Necesito un médico. | I need a doctor. |
| ¿Cada cuántas horas lo tomo? | How often do I take it? |

Many Mexican pharmacies have a small doctor's office next door (a **consultorio**) for quick, inexpensive visits.

### Emergencies

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡Ayuda! | Help! |
| Llame al 911, por favor. | Call 911, please. |
| Necesito una ambulancia. | I need an ambulance. |
| Me robaron el celular / la cartera. | My phone / wallet was stolen. |
| Estoy perdido (perdida). | I am lost. |
| ¿Dónde está la embajada de Estados Unidos? | Where is the US embassy? |

⚠️ **Watch out:** The emergency number in Mexico is **911**. Save your embassy's contact details before you go. US citizens can find them on [travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/).

## A taco guide: what to order and how

Taco stands (taquerías and street stands) are where your Spanish gets the most practice. Order by number and filling: "Me da tres de pastor." "Con todo" usually means with onion and cilantro (and pineapple for pastor). At many stands you pay at the end: tell them how many you had and ask "¿Cuánto le debo?" (What do I owe you?).

| Taco | What it is | Famous in | How to order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al pastor | Marinated pork cooked on a vertical spit (trompo), often with pineapple. Inspired by shawarma brought by Lebanese immigrants. | Mexico City and central Mexico | "Me da tres de pastor con todo, por favor." |
| Suadero | Thin cut of beef slow-cooked in fat on a round griddle until tender. | Mexico City | "Dos de suadero, por favor." |
| Carnitas | Pork slow-cooked in lard until soft, with crispy bits. Order by cut: maciza (lean), cuerito (skin), surtida (mixed). | Michoacán | "Tres de carnitas, maciza, por favor." |
| Barbacoa | Meat slow-cooked in an underground pit wrapped in maguey leaves. Usually lamb in central Mexico, beef in the north. A weekend morning food, often with consomé (broth). | Hidalgo, State of Mexico | "Dos de barbacoa y un consomé chico." |
| Birria | Goat or beef stewed with dried chiles. In Tijuana style, the tortilla is dipped in the broth and grilled, often with cheese. | Jalisco; Tijuana for the beef style | "Tres de birria con consomé, por favor." |
| Cochinita pibil | Pork marinated in achiote and sour orange, slow-roasted in banana leaves, topped with pickled red onion. | Yucatán | "Dos de cochinita. ¿La salsa de habanero pica mucho?" |
| Baja fish tacos | Battered, fried fish with cabbage, a creamy sauce, and salsa. | Ensenada, Baja California | "Dos de pescado, por favor." |
| Campechanos | A mix of meats, usually beef with chorizo or longaniza. | Mexico City | "Uno campechano, por favor." |
| Tacos de canasta | Soft, steamed "basket tacos" with fillings like potato, beans, or chicharrón, sold from baskets in the morning. | Mexico City | "Me da cuatro: dos de papa y dos de frijol." |

💡 **Tip:** Ask "¿Qué salsa pica menos?" (Which salsa is the least spicy?). Green is not always milder than red, so always ask.

## What things cost (rough 2026 ranges)

These are rough 2026 ranges in Mexican pesos (MXN). Prices vary by city: Mexico City, Cancún, Los Cabos, and Tulum cost more than smaller cities.

| Item | Rough price |
|---|---|
| One street taco | 15 to 40 pesos |
| Set lunch (comida corrida) at a local spot | 80 to 200 pesos |
| Dinner at a mid-range restaurant, per person | 300 to 800 pesos |
| Coffee at a café | 40 to 90 pesos |
| Bottle of water at a convenience store | 15 to 30 pesos |
| Beer at a bar | 40 to 120 pesos |
| Metro ride in Mexico City | under 10 pesos |
| Short ride with Uber or DiDi in a city | 60 to 200 pesos |
| Hostel bed, per night | 250 to 700 pesos |
| Mid-range hotel room, per night | 900 to 3,000 pesos |

## Money tips

- **Carry cash.** Street food, markets, small shops, and many taxis are cash only. Restaurants and chains usually take cards.
- **Carry small bills.** A 500-peso bill at a taco stand causes pain. Break big bills at supermarkets or OXXO.
- **OXXO is everywhere.** This convenience store chain sells water, snacks, and phone credit, and you can pay some bills there.
- **Use ATMs inside banks or malls.** If the ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, choose pesos. The conversion rate is usually worse.
- **Tipping (propina):** 10 to 15% in sit-down restaurants. Ask "¿La propina está incluida?" if you are not sure. Tip a few pesos to supermarket baggers, gas station attendants, and hotel staff who help with bags.
- **Gas stations:** attendants pump for you. Check that the pump starts at zero: "¿Está en ceros?"

## Mexican slang travelers hear

| Slang | Meaning | Example | When not to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| güey (also wey) | dude, guy | "¿Qué pasó, güey?" | With strangers, older people, or in formal settings. It can also be an insult. |
| ¿Qué onda? | What's up? | "¿Qué onda? ¿Cómo estás?" | With hotel staff, police, or older people. Use "Buenos días" instead. |
| chido | cool, great | "¡Qué chido tu sombrero!" | In formal situations. Fine with friends. |
| padre | cool, great | "¡Qué padre!" | Safe almost anywhere. It is a good one to use. |
| ahorita | right now... or later... or never | "Ahorita vengo." | Fine to use, but do not trust it as a time. |
| neta | truth; for real | "¿Neta?" (Really?) | In formal situations. |
| ¡aguas! | Watch out! | "¡Aguas con el coche!" | It is fine to use, and very useful to understand. |
| no manches | no way, come on | "¡No manches! ¿En serio?" | With older people or at work. Its cousin "no mames" is vulgar. |

## Culture tips

- **Greet first.** Say "buenos días" or "buenas tardes" when you enter a shop, a waiting room, or an elevator. Starting with a question and no greeting feels rude.
- **Say "provecho".** When you walk past people eating, or leave a restaurant, say "provecho" (enjoy your meal). People will say "gracias" back.
- **Use "usted" with older people and officials.** When in doubt, use usted. People will tell you if you can use tú.
- **Soften requests.** "¿Me da...?" and "¿Me podría...?" sound polite. "Quiero..." can sound blunt.
- **Say "con permiso"** when you pass someone, and "disculpe" to get attention.
- **Time is flexible for parties, not for tours.** Arriving 20 to 30 minutes late to a dinner at someone's home is normal. Tours, buses, and business meetings start on time.
- **Drink bottled or filtered water.** Most locals do too.
