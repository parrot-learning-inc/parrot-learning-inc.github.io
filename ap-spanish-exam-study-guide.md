# AP Spanish Language and Culture exam study guide (2026)

This AP Spanish Language and Culture exam study guide covers the exam format, the 6 course themes, a step-by-step playbook for each free-response task, a phrase bank, and an 8-week plan. Half the exam is multiple choice and half is free response, and the free-response tasks reward preparation more than talent.
{: .lede }

Formats can change, so always check the current format on [AP Central](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-spanish-language-and-culture), including whether your school gives the exam digitally or on paper.

## Exam format at a glance

The exam takes about 3 hours. This is the format as published in recent years. Check the current format on AP Central before you plan.

| Section | Task | Questions | Time | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I: Multiple choice | Part A: Interpretive communication, print texts | about 30 | about 40 min | about 23% |
| I: Multiple choice | Part B: Interpretive communication, print and audio texts (combined) and audio texts | about 35 | about 55 min | about 27% |
| **Section I total** | | **about 65** | **about 1 hr 35 min** | **50%** |
| II: Free response | Interpersonal writing: email reply | 1 | 15 min | 12.5% |
| II: Free response | Presentational writing: argumentative essay | 1 | about 55 min (read and listen to sources, then about 40 min to write) | 12.5% |
| II: Free response | Interpersonal speaking: conversation | 5 responses | 20 sec per response | 12.5% |
| II: Free response | Presentational speaking: cultural comparison | 1 | 4 min to prepare, 2 min to speak | 12.5% |
| **Section II total** | | **4 tasks** | **about 1 hr 30 min** | **50%** |

Each free-response task is scored on a 0 to 5 scale. There is no penalty for wrong answers in the multiple-choice section, so answer every question.

## The 6 course themes

Every text, audio, and prompt on the exam connects to one of these themes. Build vocabulary and examples for each.

| Theme | In Spanish | Example topics |
|---|---|---|
| Families and Communities | Las familias y las comunidades | Family structure, traditions, citizenship, friendship |
| Personal and Public Identities | Las identidades personales y públicas | Bilingualism, national identity, heroes, belonging |
| Beauty and Aesthetics | La belleza y la estética | Architecture, visual arts, music, literature, fashion |
| Science and Technology | La ciencia y la tecnología | Social media, access to technology, health care, ethics |
| Contemporary Life | La vida contemporánea | Education, careers, travel, free time, holidays |
| Global Challenges | Los desafíos mundiales | Environment, economic issues, health, human rights |

## Free-response playbook

### Task 1: Email reply (15 minutes)

You read an email and write a formal reply. To score well, you must greet, answer **every** question, **ask for more details** about something in the email, and close. Use **usted** throughout.

**Template:**

> Estimada señora Gómez:
>
> Le agradezco mucho su mensaje sobre [tema]. Me alegra tener la oportunidad de participar.
>
> Con respecto a su primera pregunta, [respuesta con un detalle]. En cuanto a su segunda pregunta, [respuesta con un ejemplo].
>
> Antes de terminar, quisiera hacerle una pregunta: ¿podría decirme [pregunta sobre algo del mensaje]?
>
> Quedo a la espera de su respuesta.
>
> Atentamente,
>
> [Nombre]

| Use | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Estimado señor Pérez: / Estimada señora Gómez: | Dear Mr. Pérez / Dear Mrs. Gómez (note the colon) |
| Opening | Le escribo para responder a su mensaje sobre... | I am writing to reply to your message about... |
| Thanks | Le agradezco su interés en... | Thank you for your interest in... |
| Answering | Con respecto a... / En cuanto a... | Regarding... / As for... |
| Asking | Me gustaría saber más sobre... | I would like to know more about... |
| Asking | ¿Podría explicarme...? | Could you explain to me...? |
| Closing | Quedo a la espera de su respuesta. | I look forward to your reply. |
| Sign-off | Atentamente, / Cordialmente, / Saludos cordiales, | Sincerely, / Cordially, / Best regards, |

### Task 2: Argumentative essay (about 55 minutes)

You get a question and three sources: usually an article, a chart or table, and an audio clip (played twice). You then write an essay that takes a clear position and uses all three sources.

**Structure (4 or 5 paragraphs):**

| Paragraph | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Restate the question in your own words. Give your thesis: "En mi opinión, ... porque ..." Mention that the sources support different views. |
| Body 1 | Your first argument, with evidence from one or two sources. |
| Body 2 | Your second argument, with evidence from the remaining source. |
| Body 3 (counterargument) | Present the other side fairly, cite a source, then explain why your position is still stronger. |
| Conclusion | Restate your thesis with new words and end with a final thought. |

**Citing sources:**

- Según la Fuente 1, ...
- Como muestra el gráfico de la Fuente 2, ...
- Los datos de la Fuente 2 indican que ...
- En la grabación (Fuente 3), la experta afirma que ...
- Aunque la Fuente 1 sostiene que ..., la Fuente 3 demuestra que ...

🎯 **Key point:** Cite all three sources, and do not just summarize them. Use them as evidence for your opinion. A clear thesis plus three integrated sources is the core of a high essay score.
{: .key }

### Task 3: Conversation (5 turns, 20 seconds each)

You see an outline of a conversation (for example, a friend inviting you somewhere). You hear each turn and have 20 seconds to respond. You cannot replay.

**Strategies:**

1. **Read the outline in the preview time.** Note the register (tú or usted) and what each turn asks you to do: accept, decline, suggest, explain, give details.
2. **Answer, add a detail, add a reason.** 20 seconds is longer than it feels. Fill it.
3. **Do not stop if you miss something.** Use the outline to guess and respond politely. Silence scores worse than a slightly off answer.
4. **Use fillers to start.** "Pues, mira...", "Bueno, la verdad es que...", "A ver, déjame pensar..."

| Purpose | Spanish |
|---|---|
| Greet | ¡Hola! ¡Qué gusto saber de ti! |
| Accept | ¡Claro que sí! Me encantaría. |
| Decline politely | Lo siento mucho, pero no puedo porque... |
| Suggest | ¿Qué te parece si...? / ¿Por qué no...? |
| Give an opinion | Para mí, sería mejor... porque... |
| Ask for details | ¿A qué hora...? ¿Dónde nos vemos? |
| Recover | Perdona, no estoy seguro de entender, pero creo que... |
| Close | Bueno, tengo que irme. ¡Nos vemos pronto! |

For more fillers and reactions, see our [conversational Spanish](/conversational-spanish/) page.

### Task 4: Cultural comparison (4 minutes to prepare, 2 to speak)

You compare a topic in your community with a specific Spanish-speaking community you know about. You must speak for 2 minutes.

**Structure:**

| Part | About | Example opening |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Name the topic and both communities. | Hoy voy a comparar las celebraciones familiares en mi comunidad, Houston, con las de Oaxaca, México. |
| Your community | One or two specific details. | En mi comunidad, la mayoría de las familias... |
| Spanish-speaking community | One or two specific details you know. | En Oaxaca, en cambio, he aprendido que... |
| Comparison | One similarity and one difference. | Al igual que en Houston, ... Sin embargo, a diferencia de... |
| Conclusion | Answer the prompt's question directly. | En conclusión, ambas comunidades... |

💡 **Tip:** Prepare 5 or 6 "go-to" cultural examples in advance (see the table below), each linked to two or three themes. On exam day, you only need to adapt one, not invent one.
{: .tip }

## Phrase bank: transitions and opinions

| Function | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Start | En primer lugar / Para empezar | First / To begin |
| Add | Además / Asimismo / Por otra parte | Also / Likewise / On the other hand |
| Contrast | Sin embargo / No obstante / En cambio | However / Nevertheless / In contrast |
| Concede | Aunque / Si bien es cierto que... | Although / While it is true that... |
| Cause | Debido a / Ya que / Puesto que | Due to / Since / Given that |
| Result | Por lo tanto / Por eso / Como resultado | Therefore / That is why / As a result |
| Example | Por ejemplo / Es decir / Como muestra... | For example / That is / As ... shows |
| Opinion | En mi opinión / Desde mi punto de vista / Creo que... | In my opinion / From my point of view / I think that... |
| Opinion + subjunctive | No creo que... / Dudo que... / Es importante que... | I do not think that... / I doubt that... / It is important that... |
| Agree | Estoy de acuerdo con... | I agree with... |
| Disagree | No estoy de acuerdo con... | I do not agree with... |
| Conclude | En conclusión / En resumen / Para concluir | In conclusion / In summary / To conclude |

Phrases like "no creo que" and "es importante que" need the subjunctive ("No creo que **sea** justo"). If that is shaky, review Parrot's [Spanish subjunctive guide](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/spanish-subjunctive?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=ap-spanish-exam-study-guide) and our [Spanish grammar](/spanish-grammar/) cheat sheet.

## 20 cultural comparison topic ideas

| Topic | Spanish-speaking example | Contrast idea |
|---|---|---|
| Honoring the dead | Día de Muertos in Mexico: ofrendas, marigolds, family visits to cemeteries | Halloween or Memorial Day in the US |
| Coming of age | The quinceañera in Mexico and much of Latin America | Sweet sixteen, prom, graduation |
| Meals and time | Sobremesa in Spain: staying at the table to talk after a meal | Quick meals and eating on the go |
| Daily schedule | Late dinners in Spain, often around 9 or 10 p.m. | Dinner around 6 p.m. |
| Public transport | Metrocable in Medellín, Colombia, linking hillside neighborhoods | A car-dependent suburb |
| Healthy cities | Ciclovía in Bogotá: major streets closed to cars on Sundays and holidays | Weekend habits in your city |
| Environment | Costa Rica's national parks and mostly renewable electricity | Your state's energy and parks |
| Architecture | Gaudí's Sagrada Família in Barcelona | A landmark building in your city |
| Public art | Mexican muralism (Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco) | Murals or public art where you live |
| Music and identity | Tango in Buenos Aires | A music style tied to your region |
| Bilingualism | Paraguay: Spanish and Guaraní are both official languages | Bilingual communities in the US |
| Indigenous languages | Quechua in Peru and Bolivia | Native American language revitalization |
| Education | Tuition-free public universities in Argentina | College costs and student loans |
| Technology in schools | Plan Ceibal in Uruguay: a laptop for every public school student | Technology access in your school |
| Health care | Spain's public health system | How health care works in your community |
| Food and identity | Ceviche and the rise of Peruvian cuisine | A food your region is known for |
| Sports and community | Fútbol club culture in Buenos Aires (Boca Juniors, River Plate) | High school or college sports |
| National heroes | Simón Bolívar in Venezuela and other South American countries | A national hero in your country |
| Religious festivals | Semana Santa processions in Seville | Easter or other holidays where you live |
| Leaving home | Young adults in Spain often live with their parents into their late twenties or later | Moving out at 18 for college or work |

⚠️ **Watch out:** Never compare with "Latin America" or "Hispanic culture" as a whole. Name one city, region, or country. The prompt asks for a specific community, and vague answers lose points.

## 8-week study plan

Do 30 to 45 minutes on weekdays. Add 15 minutes of Spanish listening every day (podcasts, videos, news).

| Week | Focus | Tasks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnose | Take a full released exam from AP Central under timing. Score it with the official scoring guidelines. Note your weakest task. |
| 2 | Email reply | Write 4 timed emails (15 min each). Check: greeting, all questions answered, question asked, closing, usted. |
| 3 | Argumentative essay | Write 2 full timed essays. Practice citing all three sources. Memorize the citation phrases. |
| 4 | Conversation | Do 5 practice conversations with a timer. Record yourself. Fill all 20 seconds every time. |
| 5 | Cultural comparison | Prepare your 5 or 6 go-to examples. Record 4 timed comparisons on different themes. |
| 6 | Multiple choice | Do timed print and audio sets. For every wrong answer, write down why it was wrong. |
| 7 | Mixed practice | One full Section I and one full Section II under exam timing. Review with the scoring guidelines. |
| 8 | Polish and rest | Light review of the phrase bank, your examples, and your mistake list. Sleep well the night before. |

## Common mistakes that cost points

- **Email:** using tú, skipping one of the questions, or forgetting to ask for more details.
- **Email:** no greeting or no closing. They are easy points.
- **Essay:** citing only one or two sources, or summarizing sources without taking a position.
- **Essay:** no clear thesis in the first paragraph.
- **Conversation:** going silent, or giving a 3-word answer when you have 20 seconds.
- **Conversation:** using the wrong register (usted when the outline says a friend, or tú with an adult in a formal role).
- **Cultural comparison:** talking about only one community, or using a vague "Latin America" example.
- **Cultural comparison:** stopping at 1 minute. Plan enough content for the full 2 minutes.
- **Everywhere:** English words, a narrow vocabulary, and very short simple sentences. Use connectors and some complex structures, like the subjunctive and si clauses.
- **Multiple choice:** leaving questions blank. There is no guessing penalty.
