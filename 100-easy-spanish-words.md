# 100 easy Spanish words for beginners

These 100 easy Spanish words are the ones beginners need first: greetings, people, questions, numbers, time, food, places, verbs, adjectives, and the little words that hold sentences together. Every word has a simple pronunciation guide and a real example sentence. Learn 10 a day and you know all 100 in two weeks.
{: .lede }

We picked words you will hear and use in your first real conversations. No "the cat is under the table." If you can say these 100 words, you can greet people, order food, ask where things are, and talk a little about yourself.

[Download the CSV](/data/100-easy-spanish-words.csv) with all 100 words, pronunciation, topics, and example sentences. It works in Excel, Google Sheets, Anki, and Quizlet.

## How to read the pronunciation

The "say it like" column uses English sounds. The syllable in capitals gets the stress: **hola** is OH-lah.

- Vowels never change: a = ah, e = eh, i = ee, o = oh, u = oo.
- The h is always silent. **Hotel** is oh-TEHL.
- B and v sound the same. **Vino** is BEE-noh.
- The hints follow Latin American Spanish. In most of Spain, z and c before e or i sound like the "th" in "think", so **cerveza** is thehr-BEH-thah. Both are correct.

🎯 **Key point:** Spanish is spelled the way it sounds. Learn the five vowel sounds once, and you can read almost any word out loud.

## 1. Greetings and basics

These ten words get you through a whole day of small talk. **Perdón** is for small mistakes or to get attention. **Permiso** is what you say when you need to squeeze past someone.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hola** | hello | OH-lah | ¡Hola! ¿Qué tal?<br>*Hi! How's it going?* |
| **adiós** | goodbye | ah-DYOHS | Adiós, hasta mañana.<br>*Goodbye, see you tomorrow.* |
| **sí** | yes | SEE | Sí, quiero un café.<br>*Yes, I want a coffee.* |
| **no** | no / not | NOH | No hablo mucho español.<br>*I don't speak much Spanish.* |
| **gracias** | thank you | GRAH-syahs | Muchas gracias por todo.<br>*Thank you very much for everything.* |
| **perdón** | sorry / excuse me | pehr-DOHN | Perdón, ¿dónde está el baño?<br>*Excuse me, where is the bathroom?* |
| **permiso** | excuse me (to get past) | pehr-MEE-soh | Permiso, voy a bajar.<br>*Excuse me, I'm getting off.* |
| **bien** | well / fine | BYEHN | Estoy bien, gracias.<br>*I'm fine, thanks.* |
| **claro** | sure / of course | KLAH-roh | ¿Me ayudas? Claro.<br>*Can you help me? Sure.* |
| **salud** | cheers / bless you | sah-LOOD | ¡Salud! Por los amigos.<br>*Cheers! To friends.* |

## 2. People

**Tú** is for friends, kids, and people your age. **Usted** is for older people, officials, and anyone you want to show respect to. In many Latin American countries, people use usted more often than in Spain. If you are not sure, start with usted. Nobody gets upset that you were too polite.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **yo** | I | YOH | Yo soy de Canadá.<br>*I'm from Canada.* |
| **tú** | you (casual) | TOO | ¿Tú eres de aquí?<br>*Are you from here?* |
| **usted** | you (polite) | oos-TEHD | ¿Usted habla inglés?<br>*Do you speak English?* |
| **él** | he | EHL | Él es mi hermano.<br>*He is my brother.* |
| **ella** | she | EH-yah | Ella vive en Madrid.<br>*She lives in Madrid.* |
| **nosotros** | we | noh-SOH-trohs | Nosotros comemos a las dos.<br>*We eat at two.* |
| **amigo** | friend (amiga for a woman) | ah-MEE-goh | Mi amigo se llama Luis.<br>*My friend's name is Luis.* |
| **hombre** | man | OHM-breh | Ese hombre es muy alto.<br>*That man is very tall.* |
| **mujer** | woman / wife | moo-HEHR | La mujer de la tienda es muy amable.<br>*The woman at the store is very kind.* |
| **familia** | family | fah-MEE-lyah | Mi familia es grande.<br>*My family is big.* |

## 3. Question words

Question words always carry an accent: **qué, dónde, cómo**. Without the accent, they are different words. **Porque** (one word, no accent) means "because". **Por qué** (two words, accent) means "why".

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **qué** | what | KEH | ¿Qué es esto?<br>*What is this?* |
| **quién** | who | KYEHN | ¿Quién es ella?<br>*Who is she?* |
| **dónde** | where | DOHN-deh | ¿Dónde está el hotel?<br>*Where is the hotel?* |
| **cuándo** | when | KWAHN-doh | ¿Cuándo sale el tren?<br>*When does the train leave?* |
| **cómo** | how | KOH-moh | ¿Cómo te llamas?<br>*What's your name? (literally: how do you call yourself?)* |
| **por qué** | why | pohr KEH | ¿Por qué estudias español?<br>*Why are you studying Spanish?* |
| **cuánto** | how much | KWAHN-toh | ¿Cuánto cuesta?<br>*How much does it cost?* |
| **cuál** | which / what | KWAHL | ¿Cuál es tu número?<br>*What is your number?* |

## 4. Numbers 1-10

Before a masculine noun, **uno** becomes **un**: un café. Before a feminine noun, it becomes **una**: una cerveza. That is the only change. The other numbers stay the same.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **uno** | one | OO-noh | Solo quiero uno.<br>*I only want one.* |
| **dos** | two | DOHS | Dos cervezas, por favor.<br>*Two beers, please.* |
| **tres** | three | TREHS | Tengo tres hermanos.<br>*I have three siblings.* |
| **cuatro** | four | KWAH-troh | Son las cuatro.<br>*It's four o'clock.* |
| **cinco** | five | SEEN-koh | La tienda abre en cinco minutos.<br>*The store opens in five minutes.* |
| **seis** | six | SAYS | Mi hijo tiene seis años.<br>*My son is six years old.* |
| **siete** | seven | SYEH-teh | El museo cierra a las siete.<br>*The museum closes at seven.* |
| **ocho** | eight | OH-choh | Trabajo ocho horas al día.<br>*I work eight hours a day.* |
| **nueve** | nine | NWEH-beh | El vuelo sale a las nueve.<br>*The flight leaves at nine.* |
| **diez** | ten | DYEHS | Son diez euros.<br>*It's ten euros.* |

## 5. Time

**Mañana** means both "tomorrow" and "morning". So "tomorrow morning" is **mañana por la mañana**. Yes, really.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hoy** | today | OY | Hoy hace calor.<br>*It's hot today.* |
| **mañana** | tomorrow / morning | mah-NYAH-nah | Nos vemos mañana.<br>*See you tomorrow.* |
| **ayer** | yesterday | ah-YEHR | Ayer fui a la playa.<br>*Yesterday I went to the beach.* |
| **ahora** | now | ah-OH-rah | Ahora no puedo.<br>*I can't right now.* |
| **después** | after / later | dehs-PWEHS | Te llamo después.<br>*I'll call you later.* |
| **día** | day | DEE-ah | ¡Que tengas un buen día!<br>*Have a good day!* |
| **noche** | night | NOH-cheh | Salimos esta noche.<br>*We're going out tonight.* |
| **semana** | week | seh-MAH-nah | Estoy aquí una semana.<br>*I'm here for a week.* |
| **año** | year | AH-nyoh | Tengo treinta años.<br>*I'm thirty years old.* |
| **hora** | hour / time | OH-rah | ¿Qué hora es?<br>*What time is it?* |

## 6. Food and drink

Restaurant Spanish is where most beginners use their first words. With **agua**, **café**, **por favor**, and a number, you can order almost anything.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **agua** | water | AH-gwah | Un vaso de agua, por favor.<br>*A glass of water, please.* |
| **café** | coffee / café | kah-FEH | Tomo café cada mañana.<br>*I drink coffee every morning.* |
| **comida** | food / meal | koh-MEE-dah | La comida aquí es buenísima.<br>*The food here is really good.* |
| **pan** | bread | PAHN | ¿Hay pan?<br>*Is there any bread?* |
| **carne** | meat | KAHR-neh | No como carne.<br>*I don't eat meat.* |
| **pollo** | chicken | POH-yoh | Quiero el pollo con arroz.<br>*I want the chicken with rice.* |
| **pescado** | fish (as food) | pehs-KAH-doh | El pescado está muy fresco.<br>*The fish is very fresh.* |
| **fruta** | fruit | FROO-tah | Compro fruta en el mercado.<br>*I buy fruit at the market.* |
| **cerveza** | beer | sehr-BEH-sah | Una cerveza fría, por favor.<br>*A cold beer, please.* |
| **vino** | wine | BEE-noh | ¿Vino tinto o blanco?<br>*Red or white wine?* |

## 7. Places

Pair these with **dónde** and **está**, and you can find anything: ¿Dónde está el hotel? ¿Dónde está la tienda? Add **a** for where you are going (voy a la playa) and **en** for where you are (estoy en casa). Note that a + el becomes **al**: voy al aeropuerto.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **casa** | house / home | KAH-sah | Estoy en casa.<br>*I'm at home.* |
| **calle** | street | KAH-yeh | Vivo en esta calle.<br>*I live on this street.* |
| **ciudad** | city | syoo-DAHD | Me encanta esta ciudad.<br>*I love this city.* |
| **baño** | bathroom | BAH-nyoh | El baño está al fondo.<br>*The bathroom is at the back.* |
| **tienda** | store / shop | TYEHN-dah | La tienda está cerrada.<br>*The store is closed.* |
| **restaurante** | restaurant | rehs-tow-RAHN-teh | Conozco un buen restaurante.<br>*I know a good restaurant.* |
| **hotel** | hotel | oh-TEHL | El hotel está cerca.<br>*The hotel is close.* |
| **aeropuerto** | airport | ah-eh-roh-PWEHR-toh | Voy al aeropuerto.<br>*I'm going to the airport.* |
| **trabajo** | work / job | trah-BAH-hoh | Salgo del trabajo a las cinco.<br>*I leave work at five.* |
| **escuela** | school | ehs-KWEH-lah | Mis hijos van a la escuela.<br>*My kids go to school.* |

## 8. Common verbs

These are in the infinitive, the "to" form. Here is the trick: you can use them right away with **quiero** (I want), **puedo** (I can), **necesito** (I need), or **voy a** (I'm going to). Quiero comer. Puedo hablar. Voy a ir. No conjugation needed.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ser** | to be (who or what something is) | SEHR | Quiero ser médico.<br>*I want to be a doctor.* |
| **estar** | to be (where or how something is) | ehs-TAHR | Voy a estar en casa.<br>*I'm going to be at home.* |
| **tener** | to have | teh-NEHR | Tengo hambre.<br>*I'm hungry. (literally: I have hunger)* |
| **ir** | to go | EER | Vamos a ir al cine.<br>*We're going to go to the movies.* |
| **hacer** | to do / to make | ah-SEHR | ¿Qué vas a hacer hoy?<br>*What are you going to do today?* |
| **querer** | to want / to love | keh-REHR | Quiero aprender español.<br>*I want to learn Spanish.* |
| **poder** | can / to be able to | poh-DEHR | ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?<br>*Can I pay by card?* |
| **hablar** | to speak / to talk | ah-BLAHR | Me gusta hablar con la gente.<br>*I like talking with people.* |
| **comer** | to eat | koh-MEHR | Vamos a comer algo.<br>*Let's eat something.* |
| **vivir** | to live | bee-BEER | Quiero vivir en México.<br>*I want to live in Mexico.* |
| **necesitar** | to need | neh-seh-see-TAHR | Necesito un taxi.<br>*I need a taxi.* |
| **gustar** | to like (literally: to please) | goos-TAHR | Me gusta el café.<br>*I like coffee.* |

## 9. Adjectives

Adjectives usually come after the noun, and they match it: **un café pequeño**, **una casa pequeña**. Words ending in -e (grande) or a consonant (fácil) stay the same for both.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bueno** | good | BWEH-noh | Es un libro muy bueno.<br>*It's a very good book.* |
| **malo** | bad | MAH-loh | La película es mala.<br>*The movie is bad.* |
| **grande** | big | GRAHN-deh | Es una casa grande.<br>*It's a big house.* |
| **pequeño** | small | peh-KEH-nyoh | Quiero un café pequeño.<br>*I want a small coffee.* |
| **nuevo** | new | NWEH-boh | Tengo un teléfono nuevo.<br>*I have a new phone.* |
| **caro** | expensive | KAH-roh | Este hotel es muy caro.<br>*This hotel is very expensive.* |
| **barato** | cheap | bah-RAH-toh | El mercado es barato.<br>*The market is cheap.* |
| **bonito** | pretty / nice | boh-NEE-toh | ¡Qué día tan bonito!<br>*What a nice day!* |
| **fácil** | easy | FAH-seel | Esta palabra es fácil.<br>*This word is easy.* |
| **difícil** | difficult | dee-FEE-seel | El examen fue difícil.<br>*The exam was difficult.* |

## 10. Connectors and little words

Small words, big effect. With **y**, **pero**, and **porque**, your short sentences turn into real thoughts: Es caro, pero es bueno.

| Spanish | English | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **y** | and | EE | Café y pan, por favor.<br>*Coffee and bread, please.* |
| **o** | or | OH | ¿Té o café?<br>*Tea or coffee?* |
| **pero** | but | PEH-roh | Es caro, pero es bueno.<br>*It's expensive, but it's good.* |
| **porque** | because | POHR-keh | Estudio porque quiero viajar.<br>*I study because I want to travel.* |
| **con** | with | KOHN | Un café con leche.<br>*A coffee with milk.* |
| **sin** | without | SEEN | Agua sin gas, por favor.<br>*Still water, please. (literally: water without gas)* |
| **también** | also / too | tahm-BYEHN | Yo también.<br>*Me too.* |
| **muy** | very | MWEE | Estoy muy cansado.<br>*I'm very tired.* |
| **más** | more | MAHS | Un poco más, por favor.<br>*A little more, please.* |
| **mucho** | a lot / much | MOO-choh | Me gusta mucho.<br>*I like it a lot.* |

## How to learn 100 words in 2 weeks

The plan is simple: 10 new words a day for 10 days, then 4 days of review. Each day takes about 10 to 15 minutes.

1. **Learn the new words.** Read each one out loud three times with its example sentence.
2. **Review the old ones.** Use flashcards, Spanish side first. Our free [Spanish flashcards](/spanish-flashcards/) page shows how to load lists into Anki or Quizlet.
3. **Make it about you.** Write one short sentence for each new word about your own life. "Tengo dos hermanos" sticks better than any example from a list.
4. **Hear it.** Words you hear in context stick much longer than words you only read.

| Day | New words | Review |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greetings and basics (10) | Say each word out loud in a short sentence. |
| 2 | People (10) | Day 1 words |
| 3 | Question words (8) | Days 1-2 words |
| 4 | Numbers 1-10 (10) | Days 1-3 words |
| 5 | Time (10) | Days 1-4 words |
| 6 | Food and drink (10) | Days 1-5 words |
| 7 | Places (10) | Days 1-6 words, then take the self-test |
| 8 | Common verbs (12) | Days 1-7 words |
| 9 | Adjectives (10) | Days 1-8 words |
| 10 | Connectors and little words (10) | Days 1-9 words |
| 11 | None | All 100 words with flashcards. Mark the ones you miss. |
| 12 | None | Only the words you missed. Write one sentence with each. |
| 13 | None | Watch or listen to easy Spanish and catch the words you know. |
| 14 | None | Take the self-test again. Then move to the next list. |

💡 **Tip:** Do not cram 30 words on Sunday. Ten words a day with short reviews beats one big weekly session. Words you see again after a day or two stay with you much longer.
