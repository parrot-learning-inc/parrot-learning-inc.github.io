# Spanish grammar cheat sheet

This is Spanish grammar on one page: gender, articles, the verbs you use every day, the past tenses, pronouns, and the tricky pairs. Each section is a short table you can scan in a minute. Bookmark it and come back when a sentence stops making sense.

## Nouns and gender

Every Spanish noun is masculine or feminine. The ending usually tells you which.

| Ending or type | Usually | Examples | Common exceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| -o | masculine | el libro (book), el perro (dog) | la mano (hand), la foto (photo), la radio (radio) |
| -a | feminine | la casa (house), la mesa (table) | el día (day), el mapa (map), el problema (problem) |
| -ción, -sión, -dad, -tad, -tud | feminine | la canción, la televisión, la ciudad, la libertad, la actitud | very few |
| -ma (from Greek) | masculine | el tema (topic), el sistema (system), el idioma (language) | many -ma words are not Greek: la cama (bed), la forma (shape) |
| -e or a consonant | either | el coche (car), la noche (night), el papel (paper), la flor (flower) | learn each one |
| People and animals | match the sex | el niño / la niña, el gato / la gata | la persona is always feminine |

💡 **Tip:** Learn every noun with its article: not *casa*, but *la casa*. It costs nothing now and saves you a lot of guessing later.
{: .tip }

## Articles

| | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the | el | la | los | las |
| a, some | un | una | unos | unas |

- **Two contractions:** a + el = **al** (*Voy al parque*), de + el = **del** (*la casa del profesor*).
- **Spanish uses "the" more than English:** *El café es caro* (Coffee is expensive). *Me gusta la música* (I like music).
- **Feminine nouns that start with a stressed a** take *el* in the singular: *el agua fría*, but *las aguas*.

## Plurals

| Word ends in | Do this | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| a vowel | add -s | casa → casas, café → cafés |
| a consonant | add -es | ciudad → ciudades, papel → papeles |
| -z | change z to c, add -es | lápiz → lápices, luz → luces |
| -s, unstressed last syllable | no change | el lunes → los lunes, la crisis → las crisis |

Watch the accent marks: *canción* → *canciones* (accent drops), *joven* → *jóvenes* (accent appears). A mixed group takes the masculine plural: *los padres* means "the parents".

## Adjectives: agreement and position

Adjectives match the noun in gender and number.

| Adjective type | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ends in -o | alto | alta | altos | altas |
| ends in -e | grande | grande | grandes | grandes |
| ends in a consonant | fácil | fácil | fáciles | fáciles |
| nationality | español | española | españoles | españolas |

Most adjectives go **after** the noun: *un coche rojo* (a red car). Numbers and quantity words go **before**: *dos gatos*, *muchos amigos*. A few adjectives change meaning with position:

| Before the noun | After the noun |
|---|---|
| un gran hombre (a great man) | un hombre grande (a big man) |
| mi viejo amigo (my long-time friend) | mi amigo viejo (my elderly friend) |
| un pobre chico (an unlucky boy) | un chico pobre (a boy with no money) |

Before a singular noun, *bueno* becomes *buen* and *malo* becomes *mal* (*un buen día*), and *grande* becomes *gran*.

## Subject pronouns, and tú vs usted

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | yo (I) | nosotros, nosotras (we) |
| 2nd, informal | tú (you); vos in some countries | vosotros, vosotras (you all, Spain only) |
| 2nd, formal | usted (you) | ustedes (you all) |
| 3rd | él (he), ella (she) | ellos, ellas (they) |

Spanish usually drops the pronoun, because the verb ending already shows who: *Hablo español* (I speak Spanish). *Usted* and *ustedes* use the third-person verb forms: *usted habla*.

- **Use tú** with friends, family, children, and people your age.
- **Use usted** with strangers, older people, and in formal settings. Customs vary. Spain uses tú a lot, and some Colombians use usted even with family.
- **Vos** replaces tú in Argentina, Uruguay, and parts of Central America, with its own verb forms (*vos hablás*).
- **Not sure?** Start with usted. If the other person says *puedes tutearme*, switch to tú.

## Ser vs estar

Both mean "to be". Ser is for what something is. Estar is for how or where it is.

| Use ser for | Example | Use estar for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| identity, job | Soy profesora. | location | Estoy en casa. |
| origin | Es de México. | feelings, moods | Estoy cansado. |
| traits | Ella es alta. | temporary states | La sopa está fría. |
| time, dates | Son las tres. | actions in progress | Estoy comiendo. |
| where an event happens | La fiesta es en mi casa. | results of a change | La puerta está abierta. |

Some adjectives change meaning: *ser aburrido* (to be boring) vs *estar aburrido* (to be bored), *ser listo* (clever) vs *estar listo* (ready), *ser rico* (rich) vs *estar rico* (tasty). Full guide: [ser vs estar](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/ser-vs-estar?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=spanish-grammar).

## Present tense: regular endings

Drop -ar, -er, or -ir and add the ending.

| Person | -ar: hablar (to speak) | -er: comer (to eat) | -ir: vivir (to live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | hablo | como | vivo |
| tú | hablas | comes | vives |
| él, ella, usted | habla | come | vive |
| nosotros | hablamos | comemos | vivimos |
| vosotros | habláis | coméis | vivís |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | hablan | comen | viven |

Vos forms: *hablás*, *comés*, *vivís*. For more everyday verbs, see the [basic verbs in Spanish](/basic-verbs-in-spanish/) list.

## The 10 most useful irregular verbs (present)

| Verb | Meaning | yo | tú | él, usted | nosotros | vosotros | ellos, ustedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ser | to be (what) | soy | eres | es | somos | sois | son |
| estar | to be (how, where) | estoy | estás | está | estamos | estáis | están |
| ir | to go | voy | vas | va | vamos | vais | van |
| tener | to have | tengo | tienes | tiene | tenemos | tenéis | tienen |
| hacer | to do, to make | hago | haces | hace | hacemos | hacéis | hacen |
| poder | can, to be able | puedo | puedes | puede | podemos | podéis | pueden |
| querer | to want | quiero | quieres | quiere | queremos | queréis | quieren |
| decir | to say | digo | dices | dice | decimos | decís | dicen |
| saber | to know (facts) | sé | sabes | sabe | sabemos | sabéis | saben |
| venir | to come | vengo | vienes | viene | venimos | venís | vienen |

Spot the "yo-go" pattern: *tengo, hago, digo, vengo*, and also *pongo* (poner) and *salgo* (salir).

## Stem-changing verbs

The vowel in the stem changes in every form except nosotros and vosotros.

| Change | Example verbs | yo | nosotros |
|---|---|---|---|
| e → ie | pensar (to think), empezar, querer, preferir | pienso | pensamos |
| o → ue | dormir (to sleep), poder, volver, encontrar | duermo | dormimos |
| e → i | pedir (to ask for), servir, repetir (only -ir verbs) | pido | pedimos |
| u → ue | jugar (to play), the only one | juego | jugamos |

## The near future: ir a + infinitive

This is the easiest way to talk about the future, and people use it all day.

| Form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| voy a + infinitive | Voy a llamar a mi madre. | I am going to call my mom. |
| vas a + infinitive | ¿Vas a venir? | Are you going to come? |
| va a + infinitive | Va a llover. | It is going to rain. |
| vamos a + infinitive | Vamos a ver. | We will see. / Let's see. |
| vais a + infinitive | ¿Vais a cenar aquí? | Are you all eating here? (Spain) |
| van a + infinitive | Van a llegar tarde. | They are going to be late. |

## Preterite vs imperfect

Spanish has two simple past tenses. The preterite tells what happened. The imperfect sets the scene.

| Preterite (finished events) | Imperfect (background, habits) |
|---|---|
| An action at a specific time: *Ayer comí paella.* | A past habit: *De niño, comía paella los domingos.* |
| A series of events: *Llegué, cené y me dormí.* | Descriptions, age, time, weather: *Eran las ocho y llovía.* |
| An action that interrupts: *...cuando sonó el teléfono.* | An action in progress: *Leía...* |

| Person | hablar, preterite | hablar, imperfect | comer, preterite | comer, imperfect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| yo | hablé | hablaba | comí | comía |
| tú | hablaste | hablabas | comiste | comías |
| él, usted | habló | hablaba | comió | comía |
| nosotros | hablamos | hablábamos | comimos | comíamos |
| vosotros | hablasteis | hablabais | comisteis | comíais |
| ellos, ustedes | hablaron | hablaban | comieron | comían |

-ir verbs take the -er endings in both tenses: *viví*, *vivía*. Full guide: [preterite vs imperfect](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/preterite-vs-imperfect?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=spanish-grammar).

## Por vs para

Both often mean "for". Por looks back at a cause or a path. Para looks ahead to a goal.

| Por | Example | Para | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| reason, cause | Gracias por la ayuda. | purpose, goal | Ahorro para un viaje. |
| through, along | Caminamos por el parque. | destination | Salgo para Madrid. |
| exchange, price | Lo compré por diez euros. | deadline | Es para el lunes. |
| duration | Hablamos por dos horas. | recipient | Este regalo es para ti. |
| means | Te llamo por teléfono. | opinion | Para mí, es fácil. |
| per | 100 km por hora | "considering" | Para un niño, lee muy bien. |

Full guide: [por vs para](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/por-vs-para?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=spanish-grammar).

## Direct and indirect object pronouns

| Person | Direct (it, him, her) | Indirect (to or for someone) |
|---|---|---|
| me | me | me |
| you (tú) | te | te |
| him, her, it, you (usted) | lo, la | le |
| us | nos | nos |
| you all (vosotros) | os | os |
| them, you all (ustedes) | los, las | les |

- **Before a conjugated verb:** *Lo veo* (I see it).
- **Attached to an infinitive, a gerund, or a positive command:** *Quiero verlo. Estoy haciéndolo. ¡Dímelo!*
- **Both together:** indirect comes first. *Me lo das* (You give it to me).
- In Spain, many people use *le* for a man or boy as a direct object (*le veo*). This is called *leísmo*.

⚠️ **Watch out:** *Le* and *les* become *se* before lo, la, los, or las. Say *se lo doy* (I give it to him), never *le lo doy*.
{: .warn }

## Reflexive verbs

The action goes back to the subject. The pronoun matches the subject.

| Person | Pronoun | levantarse (to get up) |
|---|---|---|
| yo | me | me levanto |
| tú | te | te levantas |
| él, usted | se | se levanta |
| nosotros | nos | nos levantamos |
| vosotros | os | os levantáis |
| ellos, ustedes | se | se levantan |

Common ones: *llamarse* (Me llamo Ana), *ducharse*, *acostarse*, *vestirse*, *sentirse*. Some verbs change meaning: *ir* (to go) vs *irse* (to leave), *dormir* (to sleep) vs *dormirse* (to fall asleep). Full guide: [reflexive verbs in Spanish](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/reflexive-verbs-spanish?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=spanish-grammar).

## Gustar and verbs like it

*Me gusta el café* means "coffee is pleasing to me". The verb matches the thing you like, not you.

| Who likes it | One thing, or an action | More than one thing |
|---|---|---|
| I | me gusta el café | me gustan los perros |
| you (tú) | te gusta | te gustan |
| he, she, you (usted) | le gusta | le gustan |
| we | nos gusta | nos gustan |
| you all (vosotros) | os gusta | os gustan |
| they, you all (ustedes) | les gusta | les gustan |

With an action, it is always singular: *Me gusta bailar.* To add stress or make it clear, add *a* + person: *A mí me gusta. A Ana le gusta.*

| Verb | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| encantar | Me encanta esta canción. | I love this song. |
| interesar | Nos interesa la historia. | We are interested in history. |
| molestar | Me molesta el ruido. | The noise bothers me. |
| doler | Me duelen los pies. | My feet hurt. |
| faltar | Me falta tiempo. | I do not have enough time. |
| importar | No me importa. | I do not care. |
| parecer | Me parece bien. | Sounds good to me. |

## The subjunctive in short

**How to form it:** take the yo form of the present, drop the -o, and use the "other" vowel. -ar verbs take -e endings. -er and -ir verbs take -a endings.

| Verb | yo, present | Subjunctive (yo, él) |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | hablo | hable |
| comer | como | coma |
| vivir | vivo | viva |
| tener | tengo | tenga |
| hacer | hago | haga |

Six common irregulars: *ser → sea, estar → esté, ir → vaya, saber → sepa, haber → haya, dar → dé*.

**When to use it:** after *que* when the first part of the sentence expresses one of these (the "WEIRDO" list):

| Trigger | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Wishes | Quiero que vengas. | I want you to come. |
| Emotions | Me alegra que estés aquí. | I am glad you are here. |
| Impersonal opinions | Es importante que duermas. | It is important that you sleep. |
| Recommendations | Te recomiendo que lo pruebes. | I recommend that you try it. |
| Doubt, denial | Dudo que llueva. | I doubt it will rain. |
| Ojalá | Ojalá ganemos. | I hope we win. |

It needs two different subjects. Compare *Quiero comer* (I want to eat) with *Quiero que comas* (I want you to eat). Also use it after *cuando* for the future: *Cuando llegues, llámame.* Full guide: [the Spanish subjunctive](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/spanish-subjunctive?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=free_resources&utm_content=spanish-grammar).

## Question words

Questions start with ¿ and end with ?. Question words always have an accent mark.

| Spanish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| qué | what | ¿Qué quieres? |
| quién, quiénes | who | ¿Quién es? |
| cuál, cuáles | which, what | ¿Cuál es tu número? |
| cómo | how | ¿Cómo estás? |
| dónde | where | ¿Dónde vives? |
| adónde | where to | ¿Adónde vas? |
| de dónde | where from | ¿De dónde eres? |
| cuándo | when | ¿Cuándo llegas? |
| cuánto, cuánta | how much | ¿Cuánto cuesta? |
| cuántos, cuántas | how many | ¿Cuántos años tienes? |
| por qué | why | ¿Por qué no vienes? |
| para qué | what for | ¿Para qué sirve? |

*Por qué* (why) is two words with an accent. *Porque* (because) is one word with no accent.

## Negation

Put *no* before the verb, and before any object pronoun: *No hablo francés. No lo sé.* Double negatives are correct in Spanish, and you need them: *No veo nada* (I do not see anything).

| Positive | Negative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| algo (something) | nada (nothing) | No quiero nada. |
| alguien (someone) | nadie (no one) | No viene nadie. |
| siempre (always) | nunca, jamás (never) | Nunca voy. = No voy nunca. |
| también (also) | tampoco (not either) | Yo tampoco. |
| alguno (some) | ninguno (none) | No tengo ninguno. |
| o... o (either... or) | ni... ni (neither... nor) | No bebo ni café ni té. |

## Spanish tense overview

| Tense | Use | hablar example |
|---|---|---|
| Present (presente) | Now, habits, facts | Hablo español. (I speak Spanish.) |
| Present progressive (estar + gerundio) | Right now | Estoy hablando. (I am speaking.) |
| Preterite (pretérito perfecto simple) | Finished past events | Hablé con ella ayer. (I talked to her yesterday.) |
| Imperfect (pretérito imperfecto) | Past habits, background | Hablaba mucho de niño. (I talked a lot as a kid.) |
| Near future (ir a + infinitivo) | Plans, the near future | Voy a hablar con él. (I am going to talk to him.) |
| Future (futuro simple) | Future, guesses about now | Hablaré mañana. (I will speak tomorrow.) |
| Conditional (condicional) | Would, polite requests | Hablaría, pero no tengo tiempo. (I would talk, but I do not have time.) |
| Present perfect (pretérito perfecto compuesto) | Past with a link to now | He hablado con ella. (I have talked to her.) |
| Past perfect (pluscuamperfecto) | Before another past event | Ya había hablado. (I had already talked.) |
| Present subjunctive (presente de subjuntivo) | Wishes, doubts, emotions | Quiero que hables. (I want you to talk.) |
| Imperfect subjunctive (imperfecto de subjuntivo) | Hypotheticals, past wishes | Si hablara más, aprendería. (If I talked more, I would learn.) |
| Imperative (imperativo) | Commands | Habla. / Hable. / No hables. (Talk. / Talk, formal. / Do not talk.) |

Spain uses the present perfect for things that happened today (*Hoy he hablado con ella*). Most of Latin America uses the preterite for this (*Hoy hablé con ella*).
